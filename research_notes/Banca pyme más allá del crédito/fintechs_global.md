# Fintechs and SME platforms in developed markets offering operational services beyond lending: case files with real results (as of Nov 2026)

Evidence labels: **[AUDITED]** = statutory or audited accounts (Companies House, 10-K). **[CO-RESULTS]** = company earnings release or results filing (SEC 8-K exhibit, ASX release). Usually unaudited for quarters. **[CO-PR]** = company press release or blog, self-reported. **[PRESS]** = journalist report. **[EST]** = third-party estimate (Sacra and similar) or aggregator. **[DERIVED]** = my own arithmetic on cited figures. Research limitation: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Later evidence came only from direct fetches of known primary URLs (SEC EDGAR, Companies House, company pages, Wikipedia). As a result, several US SME neobanks (Novo, Bluevine, Relay, NorthOne, Lili, Found, Rho) and some European players (Agicap, Libeo, Ageras/Kontist, Sage, Zettle, Aspire) could not be verified. They are listed under Gaps.

## Q1. SME neobanks and spend platforms that bundle admin tools: proposition, revenue mix, results, valuation, M&A

### Takeaway
The European SME neobanks are now large. Revolut Business has 767k customers and about 16% of a $6.0bn group revenue. Qonto has €495m of net banking income and is profitable. Tide has £235m of group revenue and is still loss-making. Their economics rest heavily on deposit/float income plus card interchange. Subscriptions are growing fastest: Tide's subscription revenue rose +92% in 2025. In the US, Mercury (deposit-led, profitable), Ramp (card/spend-led, $2bn annualised revenue, $60bn valuation) and Brex (sold to Capital One in 2026, about 58% below its peak valuation) show two different routes to scale. Bank-acquirer interest is real.

### Inferences
- **Deposit or float income is the single largest revenue line** for the e-money-licensed SME neobanks. It is about 41% of Tide's group revenue and 48% of its UK entity revenue. At Qonto, about 36% of PNB is non-commission, mostly NII. Qonto itself admitted in 2024 that it was profitable only because of NII. Any bank competing or partnering should assume these players' profitability is materially rate-dependent.
- Subscriptions are the fastest-growing line where disclosed: Tide +92% (group), Revolut +67% (all customers), Qonto commissions +22%. Subscription revenue is still small in absolute terms: Tide £28.6m is about 12% of revenue.
- The US models differ. Mercury is deposit-led and profitable. Ramp and Brex are card-interchange-led and target VC-backed startups through mid-market and enterprise. Revenue per customer is about $3k for Mercury against about $24k for Ramp [DERIVED: $2bn / 85k].
- Capital One's purchase of Brex for about $5bn shows a large bank paying for software plus card distribution rather than building it. It also shows private valuations of SME fintechs resetting sharply: down about 58% from peak.
- The UK has two strong bank-licensed digital SME players. Starling (56% SME primacy) and Monzo (business customers +45%) compete with Tide's EMI model. Tide's losses show the cost of buying international growth in India and the EU.

## Q2. Software-led SME platforms with embedded finance (accounting/AP/POS/e-commerce + payments, capital, banking)

### Takeaway
The listed software players show the clearest "money attach" economics. Square's Financial Solutions revenue (loans, instant transfer, checking interchange) reached $1.0bn in FY2025, growing +20% against +9% for commerce. Toast earns about 82% of revenue from fintech, at a roughly 23% gross margin versus about 79% on subscriptions. Shopify funded $4.2bn of merchant advances and loans in 2025. Xero paid US$2.5bn (+ up to $0.5bn earnout) for Melio to bolt AP payments onto accounting, which diluted gross margin. BILL earns about 9% of revenue from float. Pennylane (€115m ARR, €3.5bn valuation) is Europe's fastest-growing accounting-plus-finance challenger.

  - Stablecoin volume about $400bn.
  - No Capital, Treasury or Issuing metrics.
  - Source: [Stripe newsroom](https://stripe.com/us/newsroom/news/stripe-2025-update)

#### Pennylane (France): accounting platform moving into finance
- **20 Jan 2026** [PRESS]: **€175m Series E led by TCV with Blackstone Growth, at a €3.5bn valuation** (Sifted reports €3.6bn). **ARR €115m in 2025**, against €60m of sales in 2024. 800,000 business customers and 6,000+ accounting firms. - [Wikipedia]: revenue €2,804m in 2024; operating income €893m. **Acquired Holded (Spain) in June 2021**; e-conomic in 2015; Silverfin in Oct 2023. Owners: Hg 70.2%, GIC 14.2%, ICG 3.1% — [Wikipedia – Visma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visma)

### Inferences
- In the software-led models, **payments and transaction fees, not subscriptions, are the revenue engine**: Toast 82% fintech, Shopify 76% merchant solutions, BILL 73% transaction fees [DERIVED: 1,211/1,653]. These revenues carry much lower gross margins than SaaS. Xero's gross margin fell 5.1 points after adding Melio. Toast's fintech margin is about 23% against about 79% for subscriptions.
- **Capital is data-driven and repaid from sales.** Square loans are sized at under 20% of GPV and repaid as a percentage of sales; Shopify Capital bought $4.2bn; QuickBooks Capital lent $1.9bn in one quarter. Most of these platforms **originate through partner banks and sell loans forward**, which creates a natural partnership slot for banks: funding, origination charter and deposit sponsorship.
- BILL's shrinking "Embedded/FI channel" customer base (−12% year on year) suggests white-label AP distribution through banks has been less dynamic than BILL's direct channels. This is an inference from one line item.
- Pennylane (+~90% ARR growth) and Xero/Melio show accounting software moving down into payments and terminals. Software firms are also reaching the bank's "account" relationship from the software side.

## Q3. Failures, retreats and cautionary tales (causes and lessons)

### Takeaway
The verified failures share the same pattern: high cost-to-serve and credit or fraud cost in low-ARPU micro-SMEs, dependence on venture or debt funding, and partner or parent-bank strategic changes. Bench (bookkeeping-as-a-service) collapsed with $65m of debt. Brex exited tens of thousands of non-VC-backed SMBs in 2022. BBVA's Azlo was closed in 2021.

### Cited Findings
- **Bench Accounting (US/Canada, done-for-you bookkeeping)** [Wikipedia citing TechCrunch, CBC, Globe and Mail]:
  - Founded 2012. Raised over $100m in total, including a $60m Series C in 2021 led by Contour; investors include Bain Capital Ventures, iNovia and Silicon Valley Bank. About 650 staff.
  - **Shut down abruptly on 27 Dec 2024.** By end-2024 it was **in default on more than $65m of debt**.
  - Filed for bankruptcy in Canada on 7 Jan 2025 with **about $2.8m in cash and $65.4m in liabilities**.
  - Employer.com announced an acquisition on 30 Dec 2024.
  - No official cause is stated.
  - Source: [Wikipedia – Bench Accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_Accounting)
- **Bench aftermath** [CO blog by competitor Digits]: 613 employees were laid off without notice; customers were locked out of their data. The Employer.com deal completed 6 Jan 2025. Customers had to consent to data transfer, and an initial refund waiver was removed after backlash — [Digits](https://digits.com/blog/bench-fallout/)
- **Brex SMB cull (June 2022)** [PRESS]:
  - "Tens of thousands" of businesses without professional (VC, angel or accelerator) funding were dropped; accounts closed on 15 Aug 2022.
  - Stated reasons: different onboarding, sales and service needs, and resources diverted from core startup and enterprise clients.
  - An unnamed insider called SMB a "poor segment" because of operational, fraud and risk costs plus heavy rewards. **Brex earned most revenue from low-margin interchange.**
  - Context: $12.3bn valuation in Jan 2022.
  - Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/19/what-was-really-behind-brexs-decision-to-ditch-smbs/)
- **Azlo (US, BBVA-owned SME neobank)** [Wikipedia]: founded 2017 and launched Feb 2018 within BBVA's New Digital Businesses, using BBVA USA's licence. Free accounts with invoicing and PayPal/Square payment acceptance. **Closure announced 7 Jan 2021; all accounts closed by 31 Mar 2021.** The page gives no reason — [Wikipedia – Azlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azlo)
- **Brex's later reset** [CO]: big layoff in Jan 2024, leadership almost fully replaced and internal valuation reset before reaching operating-cash-flow breakeven in Aug 2025 — [Brex Journal](https://www.brex.com/journal/the-turnaround-is-over). It was then sold at about 58% below peak (see Q1).
- **Xero/Waddle** [Wikipedia]: Xero bought invoice-finance fintech Waddle in 2020 for A$80m and **closed the Waddle app in March 2023**. Even large platforms have failed to make embedded lending work — [Wikipedia – Xero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xero_(software))
- **Qonto/Penta** [Wikipedia]: Qonto bought German SME neobank Penta in 2022 and planned to migrate Penta customers onto Qonto by end-2023. Penta did not survive as a standalone brand — [Wikipedia – Qonto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qonto)

### Inferences
- Bench shows that **human-heavy, service-based bookkeeping at SMB price points is hard to make profitable**. When debt financing tightened, the company collapsed within days, and customers lost access to their financial records. For a bank, data portability and continuity risk in third-party tooling partners is a real conduct risk.
- Brex and Azlo show **segment exits are common when SMB economics (fraud, servicing, low balances) disappoint or the parent's strategy changes**. Azlo's closure coincided with BBVA USA's sale to PNC; that timing is my inference and was not stated on the opened page. A bank launching an SME fintech brand needs a multi-year commitment and a clear economics hurdle.
- Penta, Kontist and other sub-scale EU neobanks were absorbed by larger players. Scale matters in SME banking-plus-software.

### Gaps
- Bench's precise causes (failed fundraise or sale, lender action) are not documented on the opened pages; only the $65m debt default is.
- Azlo's official closure rationale was not found. The link to the PNC acquisition of BBVA USA is unverified.
- **Kontist** (acquired by Ageras: date and terms not verified), **Penta** (price), **Bo** (RBS), **HSBC Kinetic** and **Fintecture**: no opened sources (search budget).

## Q4. Typical revenue mixes and interest-rate sensitivity (float income)

### Takeaway
Account-led SME neobanks earn roughly 35–50% of revenue from deposits or float, about a third from interchange and payments, and only 10–15% from subscriptions. Falling rates are already visible in results: Starling's interest income fell 14%, Qonto's profit fell 36%, and Qonto's non-commission income fell. Software-led platforms are much less rate-sensitive: BILL earns 9% from float, and Toast and Shopify are payments-led.

### Cited Findings
- **Tide group 2025**: deposits income **£95.5m of £235.5m (40.6%)**, essentially flat at +2% while total revenue grew 24%. Interchange/payments 33%; subscriptions 12%; commission 13% — [Companies House Tide Holdings](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/10366510/filing-history) [AUDITED; shares DERIVED]
- **Tide UK 2025**: deposits income is **47.9%** of turnover — [Companies House Tide Platform](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09595646/filing-history) [AUDITED; share DERIVED]
- **Qonto**: "maintained total profitability thanks to Net Interest Income… focused on reaching operational profitability (excluding NII)" (2024 report) — [Qonto Activity Report 2024](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/24/01/586158/Qonto_Activity-report_2024_EN.pdf). In 2025, commissions were €317.6m of €495.4m PNB (64%) and net profit fell 36% — [BoursedesCrédits](https://www.boursedescredits.com/actualite-resultats-2025-fintech-francaise-qonto-confirme-rentabilite-voit-benefice-reculer-36-29808.php) [shares DERIVED]
- **Starling FY26**: interest income fell **£882m → £759m**, the stated driver of the lower profit. Revenue fell to £887m — [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/05/21/starling-s-saas-business-sees-revenue-surge-but-group-profits-dip/)
- **Revolut 2025 (all segments)**: interest income $1.3bn of $6.0bn (about 22% [DERIVED]), growing +23% against +67% for subscriptions and +45% for cards — [FinTech Australia reprint](https://www.fintechaustralia.org.au/community-news/revolut-reports-record-profit-of-usd23bn-for-2025-as-revenue-surges-to-usd6bn)
- **BILL FY26**: float revenue $148.4m of $1,653.2m (9.0% [DERIVED]); core revenue +16% vs total +13%, so float was a drag — [BILL 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001786352/000162828026057932/bill-20260630xexx991.htm)
- **BILL card economics**: rewards expense $94.0m was about 51% of Spend & Expense interchange revenue in Q4 FY26. Card-led spend platforms give back about half of interchange — [BILL 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001786352/000162828026057932/bill-20260630xexx991.htm)
- **Mercury**: revenue streams are deposit interest, interchange and software, with about $20bn of deposits per Sacra and no split. Mercury has applied for an OCC charter, which would let it keep the full spread instead of sharing it with partner banks — [Sacra – Mercury](https://sacra.com/c/mercury/) [EST]
- **Toast Q3 2026**: fintech (payments) is 82% of revenue at a 23.5% gross margin — [Toast 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650164/000165016426000214/tost-20260930xexhibit991.htm) [DERIVED]
- **Square FY2025**: Financial Solutions (lending fees, instant transfer, debit interchange, savings) is 12% of Square revenue — [Block 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1512673/000162828026012254/xyz-20251231.htm) [DERIVED]

### Inferences
- **Rate sensitivity is high for e-money and partner-bank SME neobanks.** On Tide's group numbers, a 100bp fall in the rate earned on member funds, with a flat deposit base, could cut deposit income by roughly a fifth to a quarter. This is a rough sensitivity: it assumes a ~4–5% gross yield, which is not disclosed. Tide is already loss-making, so a fall of that size would materially widen losses unless subscriptions keep growing about 90% a year.
- Players are actively **pivoting toward rate-insensitive fees**: Tide subscriptions +92%, Qonto modular add-ons, Revolut subscriptions +67%. They are also pursuing **banking licences** to keep more of the spread: Qonto (ACPR), Mercury (OCC). Banks already hold the licence and deposit franchise, which is a structural advantage as rates fall.
- For a bank, the comparable "beyond credit" revenue pool is therefore mainly interchange, payment and acquiring take rate, and transaction fees. Subscriptions are a smaller, high-margin add-on.

### Gaps
- No player in the set discloses a formal NII sensitivity (e.g., ±100bp) for SME deposits. The sensitivity above is illustrative only.
- No interchange/software/interest split for Mercury, Ramp or Brex from opened primary sources.

## Q5. Evidence on SME willingness to pay for bundled tools (paid tiers, ARPU)

### Takeaway
SMEs pay for tools, but modestly. Tide's subscription revenue is about £17 per member per year at group level. Xero's ARPC is NZ$55 per month, with churn of 1.14% per month. Paid uptake rises when tools sit inside the account workflow (Tide, Qonto add-ons) or when payments are monetised through take rate rather than seat fees (Melio, BILL, Toast).

### Cited Findings
- **Tide**: subscription revenue £28.6m in 2025 (+92%) against 1.7m+ members in Dec 2025, or about **£17 per member per year** [DERIVED]. In the UK: £25.9m against 750k+ members, about **£35 per member per year** [DERIVED]. The accounts cite the "rollout of new subscriptions plans in the UK" — [Companies House Tide Holdings](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/10366510/filing-history), [Tide Platform](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09595646/filing-history). Invoice Assistant is priced at £5.99/month + VAT — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/tide/) [EST]
- **Qonto**: commissions (subscriptions + card) €317.6m (+22%) — [BoursedesCrédits](https://www.boursedescredits.com/actualite-resultats-2025-fintech-francaise-qonto-confirme-rentabilite-voit-benefice-reculer-36-29808.php). Qonto moved invoicing onto all plans and turned advanced tools into paid add-ons "boosting value perception and driving revenue growth" (Oct 2024) — [Qonto Activity Report 2024](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/24/01/586158/Qonto_Activity-report_2024_EN.pdf)
- **Xero FY26**: **ARPC NZ$55.44 per month (+23%)**, International ARPC NZ$63.74, ANZ ARPC NZ$48.89, **churn 1.14% per month**, customer LTV NZ$21.0bn. Xero is launching an "Ultra" top tier for mid-sized businesses — [Xero ASX release](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260514/pdf/06zkqzlkq0xnph.pdf)
- **Starling**: 56% of SME customers use Starling as their primary bank; ARPAU £275 — [Starling](https://starlingbank.com/news/starling-delivers-fifth-year-of-profitability-and-accelerates-global-growth-strategy). Starling bought Ember (accounting/tax), which indicates a bank adding software to the SME account.
- **Monzo**: 1.6m+ customers on paid subscriptions (all segments); ARPU £183 — [Monzo AR 2026](https://monzo.com/annual-report/2026)
- **Revolut**: subscriptions $936m (+67%) across all customers; about $1,250 revenue per business customer [DERIVED] — [FinTech Australia reprint](https://www.fintechaustralia.org.au/community-news/revolut-reports-record-profit-of-usd23bn-for-2025-as-revenue-surges-to-usd6bn)
- **Intuit**: QBO Accounting revenue +23% FY26, driven by higher effective prices and mix shift, while customers grew only 3% (online paying). Price-led ARPU growth is starting to strain acquisition, and FY27 investment is shifting to new-customer acquisition — [01net/Intuit](https://www.01net.it/intuit-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-results-sets-fiscal-2027-guidance/); [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-intuit-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-25/)
- **Shopify**: subscription MRR $205m (+15%) against merchant-solutions revenue +35%. Merchants pay more through take rate than through subscriptions — [Shopify 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480526000007/shop-20251231.htm)
- **Pennylane**: €115m ARR across 800k customers and 6,000 accounting firms, roughly €145 per customer per year [DERIVED; mixes firms and SMEs] — [Maddyness](https://www.maddyness.com/uk/?p=764590)

### Inferences
- Pure subscription ARPU for neobank SME toolkits stays low, at tens of pounds or euros per year per member at Tide. Total revenue per SME is in the hundreds: about £140–265 for Tide, about €660–825 for Qonto, about $1,250 for Revolut Business, about $3,250 for Mercury. It is driven by float and interchange.
- Accounting software (Xero about NZ$665 per year [DERIVED: 55.44×12]) earns higher subscription ARPU with low churn. It is now adding payments (Melio) to raise revenue per customer through take rate. **Bundling payments and AP into workflow monetises better than charging for tools alone.**
- Willingness to pay is clearest where a tool saves accountant time or is compliance-driven (MTD in the UK, e-invoicing in France/EU). Tide (MTD tools), Xero (UK growth "including early flow through MTD") and Qonto (e-invoicing by default) all point to regulation as a demand catalyst.

### Gaps
- Paid-tier penetration (% of SME customers on paid plans) is not disclosed by Tide, Qonto, Revolut Business, Monzo Business or Starling in opened sources.
- CAC and payback are not disclosed by any player in the set except indirectly (Xero LTV).
- US SME neobank pricing and uptake (Novo, Relay, NorthOne, Found, Lili) were not verified.
