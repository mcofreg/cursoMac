## 1. Size and structure of the SME banking revenue pool (global, regional; lending vs deposits vs payments vs fees)

### Takeaway
SMEs are about one-fifth of global bank revenue: roughly US$850bn in McKinsey's 2019 estimate, 21% of pools in 2025, growing about 7% a year, faster than retail or corporate. Applying 21% to McKinsey's US$6.4tn 2025 revenue gives about US$1.3tn (my calc). SME economics are deposit- and fee-heavy rather than loan-heavy: in BCG's US benchmark, the SMB loan-to-deposit ratio is about 11% (68% for consumers), about 25% of revenue is fee-based, and profit per customer is 5x+ a consumer's. No published global product split (lending/deposits/payments/fees) for SME revenue was found.

### Cited Findings
- [CONSULT] MSMEs = **21% of total banking revenue pools**, and MSME banking revenue grows **~7%/yr**, faster than retail or corporate banking (McKinsey, "Digital-led with a human touch", 11 Aug 2025, Agrawal/Havas/Iyer) — [McKinsey 2025 (karriere.mckinsey.de mirror)](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [CONSULT] 2019 estimate: SMEs ≈ **one-fifth of global banking revenues ≈ US$850bn/yr**, expected to grow ~7%/yr over seven years (McKinsey, "How banks can use ecosystems to win in the SME market", 10 Jun 2019) — [McKinsey 2019](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)
- [CONSULT] Global banking 2025: revenues before risk costs **US$6.4tn** (US$6.1tn in 2024); net income **US$1.3tn** (+7%); ROTE **11.8%** (12.4% in 2024); NIM **1.63%**. **Transaction banking & distribution = 47% of revenues and 57% of profits**; balance-sheet businesses = 53% of revenues, 43% of profits (McKinsey Global Banking Annual Review 2026 preview, 21 May 2026; full report Oct 2026) — [McKinsey GBAR 2026 preview](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-banking-annual-review)
- [CONSULT][LatAm] Same source: Brazil's NIM fell from **3.55% to 2.93%**; Nubank has **131m customers, ~30% ROE** and is LatAm's most valuable bank; Western European bank ROE rose from 10.7% to 11.6% (2024→2025) — [McKinsey GBAR 2026 preview](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-banking-annual-review)
- [CONSULT] US SMB segment: SMB deposits **>US$2tn**. For traditional regional banks the SMB **loan-to-deposit ratio ≈ 11%** (consumer ≈ 68%) and **≈25% of SMB revenues come from fee-based products**. SMB customers generate **~5x+ the profit per customer** of consumers. Benchmark: 8 of the top-25 US commercial banks, segment ~US$0.5–5m revenue; BCG, "Non-banks are winning business banking", Jun 2026 — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)
- [CONSULT] US banks earn **>US$50bn net revenue from small-business lending** (firms with US$100k–25m revenue). US SMB lending assets ≈ **US$1.7tn** (CFPB), of which **~US$500bn+** sit on bank balance sheets. BCG estimates the US SMB funding gap at **US$350–750bn** (BCG + Biz2X, "The forthcoming revolution in small business lending", Jan 2025) — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)
- [CONSULT] Cash advances, bank accounts and card issuing ≈ **US$110bn revenue in US + Europe + UK ≈ 45% of the SMB banking revenue pool** there (BCG + Adyen, 24 Oct 2022). This implies a total pool of about US$245bn for US/Europe/UK (my calc) — [BCG 2022](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2022/smb-banking-embedded-finance-potential)
- [CONSULT] Western Europe: **top-performing SME banks earn ~2x the revenue per active SME customer and ~4x the revenue per RWA** of the weakest performers (Finalta data in McKinsey 2025) — [McKinsey 2025](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [HARD/official estimate] Formal MSME finance gap: **US$5.7tn across 119 EMDEs** (= **19% of GDP, 20% of private-sector bank credit**), 2019 data. Informal firms add **US$2.1tn** of potential demand (8% of GDP). SME Finance Forum/IFC page dated 20 Feb 2026 — [SME Finance Forum](https://www.smefinanceforum.org/news/11526737)
- [HARD] SMEs ≈ **90% of businesses** and **>50% of global employment** — [World Bank SME Finance](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/smefinance)
- [CONSULT] BCG cites "some estimates" of a global SMB funding gap **>US$5tn** (SME Finance Forum) and Fed data that **~50% of US small businesses** do not get all or part of the financing they seek — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)

### Inferences
- Applying McKinsey's 21% share to its own US$6.4tn 2025 revenue gives an SME pool of roughly **US$1.3tn**. This is my arithmetic: the two figures come from different McKinsey publications and definitions may differ.
- The value of an SME client to a bank sits mainly in **operating deposits plus transaction and fee flows**, not loans (LDR ~11% in the US benchmark). Non-lending services that anchor the operating account therefore protect the most profitable part of the relationship. Industry-wide, transaction banking and distribution earn 57% of profits on 47% of revenues.
- The large finance gap in emerging markets (US$5.7tn) means better SME data from invoicing/accounting tools could expand lending as well as defend deposits. This is most relevant for LatAm.

### Gaps
- No published global or LatAm/Spain **product split** of SME revenue (lending vs deposits vs payments vs fees) was found in an openable source. McKinsey's 2019 PDF exhibits (which may contain one) could not be read; mckinsey.com returned 503.
- No LatAm-specific or Spain-specific SME revenue-pool figure was found. The IFC regional annex returned 404.
- McKinsey Global Payments Report 2025 and BCG Global Payments Report 2025 (payments revenue pool, ~US$2.5tn range) could not be accessed (404/search cap). Only the GBAR "transaction banking 47%/57%" proxy is sourced.
- No published bps value for the NIM contribution of SME operating deposits was found.

## 2. What consultants and studies say about "beyond banking" economics: retention, primacy, deposits, cross-sell, revenue uplift, cost-to-serve, willingness to pay

### Takeaway
The evidence is mostly directional and survey-based. Consultants agree that owning the SME's workflow (invoicing, AP/AR, payroll, reconciliation, cash-flow tools) protects operating deposits and primacy, because "deposits follow the workflow". Hard causal numbers on churn, deposit or NPS uplift from bank-offered tools are scarce. The best-quantified data points are survey correlations (US, 2020): SMEs that get accounting/payments services from their bank are far less likely to look for a new bank. Stated SME interest in bank-provided services is real but minority-to-moderate: 15–23% want bank help with tax, accounting or financial management, and ~64% would welcome an integrated single-platform offer.

### Cited Findings
- [CONSULT] BCG (Jun 2026): "Deposits tend to follow the workflow". When invoicing, payroll and expense management sit with non-banks, the deposit account becomes "a commodity holding tank". SMBs "rarely switch their primary bank outright. Instead, they fragment". Recommended defense: payments integration and cash management. Recommended offense: embed accounting tools, cash-flow forecasting, invoicing and expense management so the bank "becomes the system of record". Marketing should lead with operational value, not rates — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)
- [CONSULT] Top reasons SMB promoters recommend their primary bank (US, REBEX Business Banking survey, n=1,000): trust to keep money safe **35%**, low fees **28%**, good internet banking **28%**, rewards **27%**, mobile app **26%**, easy-to-understand products **25%**, "bank cares about my business" **24%**, branch staff **23%** — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)
- [CONSULT] SMBs use **six banking products on average, but only half from their primary bank**. **82%** rely heavily on digital channels, but only **1 in 3** feel their bank's digital offer meets expectations. **~40%** have considered alternative financial platforms (2024 BCG Business Banking Survey) — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)
- [CONSULT] BCG InQbate: banks' small-business digital experience **lags retail by ~5 years**. "Global Best" banks score higher partly through value-added services such as "company registration with Government, accounting integration, Business management tools" (Exhibit 2, as of Dec 2024) — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)
- [CONSULT] **64% of SMBs are interested in financial services embedded within a platform**. Embedding cash advances, accounts and cards gives software platforms a **potential revenue uplift of up to 70%** (BCG + Adyen, Oct 2022; >50 platforms, >2,000 SMB users) — [BCG 2022](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2022/smb-banking-embedded-finance-potential)
- [CONSULT] The same 2022 study, cited by BCG in 2025: **69% of SMBs would change payment processor** if it were more integrated into their systems, and **65% would switch** for better integration of embedded finance — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)
- [CONSULT] An estimated **45% of SME banking revenue pools in the US and Europe will be addressable through finance embedded in industry-vertical software by 2030**. Only **~25%** of banks in a BCG study had a solid offering of third-party financial products, and only **~10%** had moved on merchant-funded offers (embedded commerce). BCG, Jan 2024 — [BCG "$7 Trillion" Jan 2024](https://web-assets.bcg.com/d2/e4/a13ce7e640ba96a5821709fec9d7/bcg-to-seize-a-7-trillion-opportunity-banks-need-bolder-strategies-jan-2024.pdf)
- [CONSULT] BCG warning (Jan 2024): "single-product lending relationships that are acquired through embedded finance are difficult to convert to full banking relationships unless the bank has a truly distinctive cross-selling engine" — [BCG Jan 2024](https://web-assets.bcg.com/d2/e4/a13ce7e640ba96a5821709fec9d7/bcg-to-seize-a-7-trillion-opportunity-banks-need-bolder-strategies-jan-2024.pdf)
- [CONSULT] McKinsey (2025) says digitally active MSME clients have "substantially higher deposit balances" and are "much less likely to leave", without numbers. In Europe, **80% of MSME clients were digitally active at least monthly in 2024, 17pp above retail customers**. Leading European banks get **75% of small-business product sales via digital**, double the average. Banks offer **<50% of 200+ tracked digital capabilities** on average, leaders ~85%. Survey base: ~2,500 small-business owners, 7 countries, Europe & Middle East, 2024 — [McKinsey 2025](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [CONSULT] McKinsey (2025) names bank moves into "corporate treasury support, digital accounting, billing" and a leading Indian SME bank that added a **mini-ERP inside its app**. No adoption or revenue numbers are given — [McKinsey 2025](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [CONSULT] McKinsey (2019): the primary challenge business owners named (survey of >500) was finding more time for their core business. Ecosystems are a "high-risk, high-reward bet" that can "add complexity without delivering material benefits" unless treated as a source of new value rather than only acquisition or retention — [McKinsey 2019](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)
- [VENDOR-commissioned survey] Cornerstone Advisors for Autobooks (US, survey of **1,265 SMEs**, revenue US$100k–50m, Mar 2020). Key figures:
  - Only **6% of SMEs** currently get accounting/payments services from a bank, spending **US$11.3bn**.
  - Among those, only **~1 in 5** are somewhat or very likely to look for a new bank in the next year (8% very + 13% somewhat), and **58% are "very unlikely"**.
  - Among all SMEs >US$5m revenue, **~7 in 10** are somewhat or very likely to look for a new bank; in the US$1–4.99m band, 52% are.
  - **59%** of SMEs very likely to switch banks would "definitely" be interested in bank-provided accounting/payments services.
  - Cornerstone itself states: "We don't know if becoming an SME's accounting and payments provider caused a small business to change its banking allegiances" (correlation, not causation).
  - [Cornerstone/Autobooks 2020](https://content.autobooks.co/hubfs/Industry%20Reports/The%20$370%20Billion%20Small%20Biz%20Banking%20Opportunity.pdf)
- [VENDOR-commissioned survey] Cross-sell (same source): among SMEs "definitely" interested in bank accounting/payments services, **62% intend to borrow in the next two years (avg ≈ US$2.0m)**, vs 21% of the "not interested". **85%** would consider their primary bank for the loan, vs 64% of the "not interested" — [Cornerstone/Autobooks 2020](https://content.autobooks.co/hubfs/Industry%20Reports/The%20$370%20Billion%20Small%20Biz%20Banking%20Opportunity.pdf)
- [VENDOR-commissioned survey] Demand: **~4 in 10 SMEs with revenue >US$5m** would "definitely" consider a bank for accounting, invoicing, bill pay and payment acceptance. The estimated bank revenue opportunity is **~US$370bn/yr**: accounting/bookkeeping US$95.1bn, invoicing US$99.6bn, bill payment US$98.7bn, payment acceptance US$76.1bn. **44%** of the opportunity comes from SMEs with US$1–10m revenue, which are 26% of SMEs. Caveat: the report's executive summary says SMEs spend "roughly US$225bn" on these third-party services, but its Figure 2 totals **US$531bn**, an internal inconsistency — [Cornerstone/Autobooks 2020](https://content.autobooks.co/hubfs/Industry%20Reports/The%20$370%20Billion%20Small%20Biz%20Banking%20Opportunity.pdf)
- [VENDOR-commissioned survey] Pain points (same source): accounting is "manual and labor-intensive" for **57%** of SMEs and not integrated with other financial functions for **48%**; invoicing is manual for **54%**. Owners spend **~18.4 h/week** (CEOs ~24.1 h/week) on accounting and payments. DSO is **~28–29 days** for firms <US$5m, rising to **~41** for US$20–50m firms — [Cornerstone/Autobooks 2020](https://content.autobooks.co/hubfs/Industry%20Reports/The%20$370%20Billion%20Small%20Biz%20Banking%20Opportunity.pdf)
- [CONSULT, secondary citation] BCG "What SMEs Really Want" (Sep 2024), as quoted by SME Banking Club (1 Oct 2026): **23%** of SMEs want bank support with taxation, **19%** with accounting & reporting, **15%** with financial management; **64%** would welcome an integrated offering through a single platform. The original BCG document was not opened — [SME Banking Club, Oct 2026](https://smebanking.club/beyond-banking-for-smes-why-e-invoicing-and-accounting-services-are-becoming-the-new-battleground-in-european-banking/)
- [3P] Qonto (subscription-based SME neobank with invoicing and bookkeeping): **78% of customers use Qonto as their primary account** (Sep 2025, podcast source cited by Contrary Research) — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/qonto)
- [CONSULT] Cost to serve: advanced analytical credit decisioning and monitoring typically save **30–40% of end-to-end credit-process cost** (BCG experience). Leading banks should be able to automate decisions on loans **up to US$1m** in North America — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)

### Inferences
- The best-supported business-case mechanism is **defensive**: protecting operating deposits and fee flows that are at risk of fragmentation (BCG 2026; McKinsey 2025). Offensive revenue from tool subscriptions is secondary and small per client (see Q5).
- The Cornerstone retention correlation (≈21% vs ≈50–70% "likely to look for a new bank") is the most-quoted quantified link, but it is **2020, US, vendor-commissioned and non-causal**. The board document should present it as indicative only.
- Willingness to buy these services from a bank is concentrated in **larger small businesses (>US$1–5m revenue)**. Micro-enterprises show lower stated interest, and their spend is mostly internal time rather than paid tools.

### Gaps
- No original, published causal estimate of churn reduction, deposit uplift (%), NPS uplift or credit-loss reduction **from bank-offered accounting/invoicing tools** was found in an openable primary source. Commonly circulated figures such as "X% lower churn" could not be traced to an original study in this session.
- No LatAm or Spain survey of SME willingness to pay a bank for these services, or at what monthly price, was found.
- Bain SME-banking NPS/loyalty data were not reached (search budget exhausted).

## 3. Credit-risk benefits of real-time accounting, invoice and payment data (approval rates, default rates)

### Takeaway
Academic evidence consistently finds that verifiable transaction and payment data improves SME screening: higher approval, lower pricing, lower default conditional on price. The best-quantified study is Indian fintech data (Journal of Finance, 2026), where an interquartile rise in a firm's cashless-payment share raises approval by ~2.7–3pp (≈10% of baseline) and cuts the offered rate by ~42bp. BIS work on Mercado Libre (Argentina) shows platform data beat bureau scores at predicting losses. However, BIS (2025) cautions that data alone does not close EM SME credit gaps.

### Cited Findings
- [ACAD] Ghosh, Vallee & Zeng, "FinTech Lending and Cashless Payments" (NBER WP 34148, Aug 2025; *Journal of Finance* 81(2): 1053–1101, 2026). Borrowers who use more cashless payments get better access to capital and are less likely to default. Effects are stronger for payment technologies that give more precise information. The authors say the synergy "supports open banking" — [NBER w34148](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34148)
- [ACAD] The same study's working-paper version (Aug 2021) uses Indian fintech lender Indifi, Sep 2015–Sep 2019:
  - **86,447 applications**, baseline **approval 25.2%**, average offered rate **24.8%**, **default 7.3%** among 9,138 loans with outcomes.
  - An interquartile increase in cashless-payment share (31pp) is associated with **~2.7–3pp higher approval probability (>10% of baseline)** and a **~42bp lower interest rate**. One sentence in the text says "lower" for approval, which contradicts the abstract and intro and appears to be a typo.
  - A higher cashless share predicts **lower default conditional on the interest rate**.
  - [Wharton WP 2021](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Zeng_2021_WP-Fintech-lending-and-cashless-payments.pdf)
- [ACAD/BIS][LatAm] Frost, Gambacorta, Huang, Shin & Zbinden, BIS WP 779 (Apr 2019). Credit models using machine learning on Mercado Libre platform data "are better at predicting losses than traditional credit bureau ratings" (Argentina). Argentine small firms using BigTech credit "offered more products and had higher sales". The paper discloses a Mercado Libre co-author — [BIS WP 779](https://www.bis.org/publ/work779.pdf)
- [BIS] Caballero, Doerr, Mehrotra & Zampolli, BIS Bulletin 99 (27 Feb 2025). Data-driven bigtech/fintech lenders can reduce SMEs' need for collateral, but their lending remains "only a small share", and "digital innovation alone may not substantially improve SME lending" without progress on deeper obstacles — [BIS Bulletin 99](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull99.pdf)
- [CONSULT] BCG (Jan 2025): SMB transaction-account data gives "a real-time view on the performance of the business, including insight into when businesses may need or benefit from financing". Open banking (and US CFPB 1033) increases data portability. Shifting to cash-flow underwriting enables more consistent underwriting — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf)
- [VENDOR-commissioned] Cornerstone (2020) argues that a deposit is "more valuable to a bank when it is applied to a small business' P&L statement", and uses Amazon's merchant cash advances (US$1bn in 2017) as an example of data-led pre-approved lending. This is argument, not measured outcome — [Cornerstone/Autobooks 2020](https://content.autobooks.co/hubfs/Industry%20Reports/The%20$370%20Billion%20Small%20Biz%20Banking%20Opportunity.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a board case, the credit benefit is best framed as: (i) better approval decisions and pricing for thin-file SMEs; (ii) earlier warning signals during monitoring; (iii) lower underwriting cost (BCG: 30–40% process savings). It should not be framed as a fixed "−X% losses", which no primary source here supports for bank-offered tools specifically.
- LatAm relevance: mandatory e-invoicing (CFDI, SII, NF-e, DIAN) means invoice data already exists in machine-readable form. This strengthens the case that a bank capturing the invoicing/AR workflow (with consent) can use it for underwriting. This is an inference; the bank-level evidence was not found.

### Gaps
- Not opened (abstracts or summaries only, seen in search results):
  - Alok, Ghosh, Kulkarni & Puri, NBER w33259 (2024): UPI digital payments expanded credit to new-to-credit borrowers, mainly via fintechs, reportedly with "no discernible increase in default rates" — [NBER w33259](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33259)
  - Rishabh (TSE/NSE), "Beyond the Bureau": payment data adds to bureau data in predicting SME delinquency.
  - A Norwegian bank study (MDPI JRFM 2022) on open-banking transaction data in scoring (403 on fetch).
  - Effect sizes for all three should be verified before use.
- No study measuring default and approval outcomes for SMEs specifically using **accounting-software ledgers or e-invoice data at a bank** was found.

## 4. SME software market and SaaS unit economics (ARPU, churn, gross margin, CAC) from public filings; SME spend on tools

### Takeaway
Public SME-software leaders show the economics a bank would compete with or partner with:
- Xero: ARPC ≈ NZ$55/month, monthly churn ~1.1%, gross margin 84–89%, adjusted EBITDA margin ~27%.
- Intuit: QuickBooks Online Accounting revenue +23% in FY26, mostly from price and mix.
- BILL: AP/AR payments platform; ~US$1.65bn revenue, 73% transaction fees, 9% float.

Tool prices for SMEs cluster at **€6–€50/month** for basic invoicing/bookkeeping. Total SME spend on outsourced accounting and payments work (bookkeepers, processors) is much larger than software spend.

### Inferences
- **Xero unit economics** (my calcs): ARPC NZ$55.44 ≈ NZ$665/yr. At Xero's own planning rate of NZ$/US$0.57, that is ≈ **US$32/month (≈US$380/yr)**. 1.14% monthly churn ≈ **~12.8% annual churn**, or an implied average life of ~7 years. LTV/customer ≈ NZ$21.0bn / 4.92m ≈ **NZ$4,300**.
- These are best-in-class SaaS benchmarks. A bank reselling a partner tool at €6–20/month would earn only a share of that, so **tool revenue alone is unlikely to move a bank P&L**. The value is in deposits, payments and credit.
- **Margin mix**: Xero's gross margin fell 5.1pp after it added payments (Melio). BILL gives back 51% of card interchange as rewards. Payments and float revenue therefore carry lower margins and more rate sensitivity than pure software.

## 5. Revenue models available to banks and published examples

### Takeaway
Banks use five models:
1. **Free, bundled and conditional on the account**: NatWest/FreeAgent, free while the business current account is kept.
2. **Paid partner software distributed by the bank**: CaixaBank/Quipu at €9.56–19.96/month, provided by the partner.
3. **Tiered subscription business accounts**: Qonto €9–199/month; Tide, Starling and Revolut add-ons £6–£125/month.
4. **Bundling into acquiring/POS**: CaixaBank's TPV includes the Verifactu module free.
5. **Monetizing float, interchange and credit referral**: BILL float 9% of revenue; Tide earns from partner loans.

Published revenue splits are rare. BILL (73% transaction fees / 18% subscription / 9% float) is the cleanest public example of an AP/AR model.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD] **NatWest Group (UK)**: FreeAgent accounting is **free "as long as you retain your bank account"** (NatWest, RBS, Ulster). Mettle requires **≥1 transaction/month**. One free licence per eligible account; optional add-ons (Unlimited Smart Capture, Amazon connection) are chargeable. Non-NatWest users get a 30-day trial, then 50% off for six months — [FreeAgent pricing page](https://www.freeagent.com/pricing/free-accounting-software/)
- [HARD][ES] **CaixaBank**: TPV Tablet "Incluye el módulo Verifactu de forma gratuita". Quipu invoicing is resold at €9.56/€19.96 per month and provided by the partner — [CaixaBank](https://www.caixabank.es/empresa/bancadistancia/verifactu_es.html)
- [CONSULT] McKinsey (2019) describes the orchestrator model: banks charge subscriptions (€6–15/month in one European example), take **a share of third-party provider fees** (no % given), and spend **€5–20m to build an MVP** — [McKinsey 2019](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)
- [3P] **Tide (UK/India/DE/FR)**: free core account as acquisition engine; subscription add-ons (Invoice Assistant £5.99+VAT; Admin Extra plans); Tap-to-Pay acquiring at **1.65%**; credit via partners (Iwoca loans up to £150k) and in-house Credit Flex (up to £5k) — [Sacra – Tide](https://sacra.com/c/tide/)
- [HARD] Tide scale (27 May 2026): **>2m members** globally, **900k UK SMEs = 15% UK SME market share**, >1.1m in India, **>£1.75bn** UK credit facilitated, >€50m disbursed in DE/FR via credit intermediation. Valuation US$1.5bn after TPG investment (late 2025); the UK generates most revenue — [City AM, 27 May 2026](https://www.cityam.com/tide-crosses-2-million-members-worldwide-big-step-forward-in-mission-to-support-and-grow-small-businesses/)
- [HARD] **BILL** revenue mix FY26: subscription 17.8%, transaction fees 73.3%, float 9.0% (my calc from US$293.5m / US$1,211.2m / US$148.4m) — [BILL 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001786352/000162828026057932/bill-20260630xexx991.htm)
- [HARD] **Xero** AMRR growth was driven by customer growth, ARPC expansion and "an increase in payments revenue, which includes Melio's revenue contribution". Xero now calls itself a "small business financial operating system" uniting accounting and payments — [Xero FY26 release](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260514/pdf/06zkqzlkq0xnph.pdf)
- [HARD] **Intuit**: Online Services growth was driven by "money" (payments and capital) and payroll offerings — [Intuit FY26 release](https://www.01net.it/intuit-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-results-sets-fiscal-2027-guidance/)
- [CONSULT] BCG (2026): SMB revenues are ~25% fee-based and deposit-heavy (LDR 11%); deposits are "the gateway to a much broader commercial relationship" — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)
- [3P] **Qonto**: subscription-centred model with add-ons (expense management, accounting), card fees and transaction fees. Plan range ~€9 to ~€199/month. Profitable since 2023 per the CEO (TechCrunch, Jul 2025, via Contrary) — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/qonto)

### Inferences
- **Business-case structure** (inference, synthesizing the above):
  - Direct revenue: subscription or revenue share, plus acquiring MDR, plus AR/AP transaction fees.
  - Indirect value: retained and new operating deposits valued at the funds-transfer-pricing spread, higher primacy and cross-sell, plus lower credit cost and lower underwriting opex.
  - Costs: build or partner licence, integration, marketing and activation, support.
  - The published data suggest indirect deposit and primacy value dominates; direct software fees are €6–20/month per active user.
- **Free-if-primary** (NatWest model) makes the tool a retention device whose cost should be charged against deposit and primacy value. **Paid partner resale** (CaixaBank/Quipu) limits bank cost but weakens the retention lever and data capture.
- **Rate sensitivity**: float and deposit value fall with rates. BILL's float revenue declined as rates fell (10-K excerpt in search: −US$13.4m on lower yields, unverified). LatAm's higher rates (e.g., Brazil NIM 2.93%) make the deposit leg relatively more valuable than in Europe.

### Gaps
- No bank has published a revenue split for its SME beyond-banking offer (subscription vs acquiring vs deposits). No Spanish or LatAm bank P&L disclosure for such services was found.
- NatWest's FreeAgent acquisition cost and FreeAgent user numbers via NatWest were not verified in an opened source. FreeAgent's claim that "virtually all" NatWest-origin customers took the free licence was seen only in a search snippet.
- Revenue-share percentages in bank–software partnerships are not public in any opened source.

## 6. Main cost items, failure modes and adoption/activation rates of bank-offered SME tools

### Takeaway
Published cost data are thin: McKinsey cites €5–20m for an MVP ecosystem build, and McKinsey's 2026 GBAR puts the success rate of embedded-finance initiatives at about 50%. The main failure modes are:
- added complexity without material benefit;
- IT-integration delays;
- weak organizational ownership and campaigns that do not outlive budget cycles;
- single-product relationships that never convert;
- free government and fintech alternatives.

Bank-sourced adoption was low historically: only 6% of US SMEs got accounting/payments services from a bank in 2020.

### Cited Findings
- [CONSULT] Build cost **€5–20m for an MVP**. Integrating into the mobile app caused "substantial implementation complexity" and delayed one bank's launch. Housing the initiative inside the existing SME product team "often lacks start-up focus". Data monetization is limited by GDPR. Ecosystems can "add complexity without delivering material benefits" (McKinsey 2019) — [McKinsey 2019](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)
- [CONSULT] McKinsey GBAR 2026 preview lists **embedded finance among strategic innovations with a "moderate" success rate of ~50%** — [McKinsey GBAR 2026 preview](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-banking-annual-review)
- [CONSULT] BCG (2026): banks lose SMB ground mainly because "the strategy fails to outlive a single budget cycle". BCG recommends permanent ownership and board-level tracking of deposit health "the same way they treat credit risk" — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)
- [CONSULT] Few banks execute embedded commerce well: in BCG's study, only **1–2% of banks had "best" offerings** in each embedded-commerce category (Exhibit 10), ~25% had a solid third-party financial products offer, and ~10% had moved on merchant offers — [BCG Jan 2024](https://web-assets.bcg.com/d2/e4/a13ce7e640ba96a5821709fec9d7/bcg-to-seize-a-7-trillion-opportunity-banks-need-bolder-strategies-jan-2024.pdf)
- [VENDOR-commissioned survey] Adoption baseline: **6% of US SMEs** got accounting/payments services from a bank in 2020. Penetration was concentrated in some regionals and community banks: PNC and Citizens are primary bank for 3% of non-users but 11% of users; community banks and credit unions 9% vs 18% — [Cornerstone/Autobooks 2020](https://content.autobooks.co/hubfs/Industry%20Reports/The%20$370%20Billion%20Small%20Biz%20Banking%20Opportunity.pdf)
- [CONSULT] McKinsey (2025): banks offer **under half of 200+ tracked digital capabilities** (leaders ~85%). One European incumbent's conversion-rate optimization effectively **doubled its share of digitally acquired MSME customers** — [McKinsey 2025](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [HARD] Xero shows that activation of embedded AI features can be large on a software-native base (**500k GenAI feature users** out of 4.92m customers ≈ 10%, my calc) — [Xero FY26 release](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260514/pdf/06zkqzlkq0xnph.pdf)
- [HARD][ES] Free and cheap alternatives limit monetization in Spain. The bank-distributed tool (Quipu) is paid, and CaixaBank bundles Verifactu free only inside its POS product — [CaixaBank](https://www.caixabank.es/empresa/bancadistancia/verifactu_es.html)
- [BIS] Caution on the credit leg: "digital innovation alone may not substantially improve SME lending" — [BIS Bulletin 99](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull99.pdf)

### Inferences
- The main cost items to model are:
  - Partner licence or build: €5–20m MVP per McKinsey 2019, to be inflated to 2026 terms.
  - Core-banking and app integration.
  - Data/ERP connectors and open-banking APIs.
  - Activation marketing, onboarding and support, often the binding constraint.
  - Compliance: e-invoicing certification (Verifactu/SIF fines up to €50k for users and €150k for software producers, per CaixaBank).
- An ~50% success rate for embedded finance (McKinsey) and 6% historical penetration (Cornerstone) argue for phased investment with activation KPIs: monthly active tool users, share of AR/AP flows via the bank, and operating-deposit balances of tool users vs a matched control group.

### Gaps
- No published **activation rate** (e.g., % of eligible SME clients actively using a bank-offered invoicing/accounting tool after 12 months) for any named bank was found in an opened source.
- Named failure or exit cases (banks closing or selling SME tool businesses) could not be researched after the search budget ran out.
- No LatAm adoption data was found.

## 7. Macro "why now": e-invoicing mandates, open banking/finance, AI agents, fintech competition

### Takeaway
Four forces converge in 2026–2028:
- **E-invoicing and tax-reporting mandates**: EU ViDA has been in force since Apr 2025, with intra-EU digital reporting from Jul 2030. Spain's B2B e-invoice obligation is announced for Oct 2027 (>€8m turnover) and Oct 2028 (rest), with Verifactu also slipping to Oct 2028 per the latest announcement. LatAm clearance regimes are long established, e.g., Mexico CFDI since 2004 and mandatory since 2012.
- **Fintech share gains**: UK neobanks reach ~1/3 of MSMEs, and Tide alone has 15% of UK SMEs. Fintechs hold ~17% of combined top-bank and top-fintech revenue.
- **AI agents** in accounting platforms: Xero JAX/XeroForce, and agents that move idle cash.
- **Open-banking data portability**, which makes SME data a contested asset.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD] **EU ViDA**: adopted 11 Mar 2025, published 25 Mar 2025, in force **14 Apr 2025**. Member States may introduce mandatory domestic e-invoicing; **digital reporting for cross-border B2B from 1 Jul 2030**; national systems to align by **1 Jan 2035**. Expected impact: VAT fraud reduced by **up to €11bn/yr**; compliance cost savings of **>€4.1bn/yr over ten years** — [European Commission ViDA](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-digital-age-vida_en)
- [HARD][ES] **Verifactu (RD 1007/2023)**, per CaixaBank: **1 Jan 2027** for corporate-income-tax payers and **1 Jul 2027** for autónomos/others. Fines: **€150k** for software producers, **€50k** for users. Companies with turnover >€6m are excluded from the SIF requirement — [CaixaBank](https://www.caixabank.es/empresa/bancadistancia/verifactu_es.html)
- [ES] Conflicting, more recent source: TeamSystem's Spain country manager (Vodafone Empresas, **5 Nov 2026**) says B2B e-invoicing (Crea y Crece) will be mandatory **from 6 Oct 2027 for firms invoicing >€8m** and **from 6 Oct 2028 for the rest**. He says Hacienda has announced **postponing Verifactu to Oct 2028** to coincide, but that this "aún debe formalizarse en el BOE" — [Vodafone Empresas / TeamSystem, Nov 2026](https://www.vodafone.es/c/empresas/es/nuestra-vision/facturacion-electronica-pymes-ceo-teamsystem/). Treat dates as fluid; the writer should check the BOE.
- [HARD/secondary] Other EU mandates (SME Banking Club, 1 Oct 2026):
  - Poland KSeF mandatory from **1 Feb 2026** (sales >PLN 200m) and **1 Apr 2026** (all others); penalties from 1 Jan 2027.
  - Romania e-Factura B2B since 1 Jan 2024 and B2C since 1 Jan 2025.
  - Hungary real-time invoice reporting since 2018.
  - [SME Banking Club](https://smebanking.club/beyond-banking-for-smes-why-e-invoicing-and-accounting-services-are-becoming-the-new-battleground-in-european-banking/)
- [Secondary, Wikipedia][LatAm] Mexico: CFDI introduced in **2004**, non-standard invoices banned since **2012**, invoices stored with the tax authority. Chile implemented a similar system in **2003**, Colombia in **2020**. Brazil's NF-e and Ecuador's e-invoice are listed without dates — [Wikipedia: Electronic invoicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_invoicing). Note: the same page's France entry ("July 2024") is outdated and should not be used.
- [CONSULT] **Fintech competition, UK**: Monzo, Revolut Business, Starling and Tide count **about one-third of UK MSMEs** as customers, vs a negligible share five years earlier (McKinsey 2025) — [McKinsey 2025](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [HARD] Tide: **15% UK SME market share** (900k of ~5.2m UK SMEs), May 2026 — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/tide-crosses-2-million-members-worldwide-big-step-forward-in-mission-to-support-and-grow-small-businesses/)
- [CONSULT] **Fintech competition, US**: **49%** of MSMEs use incumbent ISVs as their primary point-of-service provider and **42%** use fintech ISVs (McKinsey US Merchant Acquiring Survey Q2 2022, n=839). ISV-managed restaurant spend grew ~2x as fast as legacy acquirers' — [McKinsey 2025](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/digital-led-with-a-human-touch-the-next-era-in-small-business-banking)
- [CONSULT] **Fintech competition, global**:
  - Fintechs hold **~17% of combined top-bank + top-fintech revenues**, up from 10%. The top 1,000 fintechs earn **US$625bn** vs US$3.8tn for the top 1,000 banks.
  - Fintech revenue grew **22%** vs 5% for banks (2021–2025 per McKinsey).
  - **21 US bank-charter applications by fintechs in 2025** (1 in 2024).
  - Revolut: 69m customers, ~35% ROE.
  - [McKinsey GBAR 2026 preview](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-banking-annual-review)
- [CONSULT] BCG (2026) timeline of non-bank expansion: 2010s core-banking fintechs; 2015–20 vertical SaaS; 2020–23 embedded-finance APIs; **2024+ "Agentic AI & Platform Wars — accounting & commerce platforms launch finance agents"**. Non-banks target operating accounts directly, e.g., OnDeck acquiring Grasshopper Bank (~US$3bn deposits) and Mercury and Revolut layering on products — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)
- [CONSULT] **AI agents and deposits**: AI agents can monitor balances, compare yields and move idle cash to higher-yield accounts (PayPal agentic wallet, Hazel AI cited) — [McKinsey GBAR 2026 preview](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-banking-annual-review)
- [HARD] AI in SME accounting: Xero JAX (>40m reconciled transactions at 97% reported accuracy; 500k GenAI users), Anthropic and OpenAI integrations, XeroForce agent builder — [Xero FY26 release](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260514/pdf/06zkqzlkq0xnph.pdf)
- [CONSULT] **Open banking**: open-banking regulation and US CFPB 1033 "will continue to promote the increased portability and interconnectivity of data" — [BCG Jan 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-forthcoming-revolution-in-small-business-lending.pdf). The Ghosh-Vallee-Zeng findings explicitly support open banking and data sharing — [NBER w34148](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34148)
- [CONSULT] **Stablecoins/tokenized deposits** are a longer-horizon threat. Only about **one-tenth of ~US$300bn** stablecoin supply is linked to real-economy payments; the largest revenue erosion is expected in transaction-banking fees rather than deposits (BCG citing its May/Jun 2026 digital-asset work) — [BCG Jun 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/white-paper-non-banks-are-winning-business-banking.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Spain**: mandatory B2B e-invoicing (Oct 2027/2028) plus Verifactu forces every SME and autónomo to adopt compliant software within ~1–2 years. That is a one-off acquisition window for banks to become the invoicing and receivables system, either free-if-primary or bundled with POS. It also compresses pricing, since software vendors and the AEAT offer free or cheap tools (AEAT free app seen only in a search snippet; not verified).
- **LatAm**: e-invoicing is already universal in Mexico, Chile and Colombia, so the differentiator is not compliance but **AR/AP automation, collections, payment links, cash-flow forecasting and invoice-based credit** on top of existing clearance data.
- **AI agents** raise both risk and opportunity. Accounting-platform agents (Xero) could become the SME's primary interface and route idle cash elsewhere, which argues for banks owning or tightly partnering the workflow layer now (BCG 2026).

### Gaps
- **France** e-invoicing calendar (understood to be Sept 2026/Sept 2027 under the 2024 Finance Law) was not verified in an opened source; Wikipedia's entry is outdated.
- Brazil, Peru and Argentina dates, and LatAm 2025–2026 reforms (e.g., Brazil's tax reform), were not verified.
- No LatAm statistic on the share of SMEs using a fintech or neobank as primary bank was found in an opened source; only Nubank's total customers (131m) and ROE.
- EU SAFE survey and OECD SME Financing Scoreboard 2026 data were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
