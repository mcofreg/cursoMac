## Q1. Brazil: StoneCo, PagBank, Cora, Conta Azul, Omie, Asaas, Nubank PJ, Mercado Pago (and others)

### Takeaway
In Brazil the money is made by the **payments-led** models: acquiring, then the account, then credit (Stone, PagBank, Mercado Pago), and by **collections-led SME accounts** (Asaas, Cora). Both have reached profitability. Asaas reports R$41m net income on R$563m gross revenue (2025). Cora has been profitable since Oct-2024 with more than 1.7m clients. Stone did the opposite of a "software-drives-banking" strategy: it **sold Linx and kept payments, banking and credit**. It credits its deposit growth to payments-to-banking cross-sell, but its credit book doubled while NPL90 nearly doubled to 8.6% (2Q26). Accounting/ERP SaaS turned out to be a valuable asset class on its own: Visma bought Conta Azul for roughly R$2bn (Aug-2025), and Partners Group invested US$100m in Omie at about US$700m (Sep-2025). Omie and Conta Azul are adding embedded finance, but finance is still a small share of their revenue (Omie about 7%).

#### Omie. SME ERP + embedded finance (account, receivables advance, card, FIDC)
- From Bloomberg Línea (11-Sep-2025) [mgmt claim/press]: [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/startups/partners-group-adquire-15-da-omie-por-us-100-milhoes-e-mira-digitalizacao-de-pmes/)
  - **Partners Group bought 15% for US$100m**, leading a US$150m Series D, mostly secondary. Implied valuation **about US$700m**.
  - **ARR close to R$600m**. Reaches **more than 28k accounting offices**. Targets 1m clients and US$1bn revenue by 2030.
  - **Financial services are about 7% of revenue**, with a target of 25-30% by 2030. These were built via acquisitions of Linker (digital bank, 2021) and Ergoncredit (2022).
  - Clients issue **more than R$35bn of NF-e per month**. More than 80% of new clients move to Omie from paper or Excel.
- Brazil Journal (1-Aug-2025) separately cites Omie **ARR "just over US$100m" and free cash flow since mid-2023**. [Brazil Journal](https://braziljournal.com/visma-compra-a-contaazul-por-quase-r-2-bilhoes/)
- From Jornal Contábil (23-Jul-2026) [press]: [Jornal Contábil](https://jornalcontabil.com.br/noticia/omie-expande-atuacao-em-servicos-financeiros-com-fidc-e-cartao-corporativo-para-pmes/)
  - About 200k clients, more than 25k partner accountants, about 1,000 employees.
  - Launched a **receivables FIDC starting at R$75m, targeting R$200m by 2027**. Omie holds the **junior quota and absorbs first loss**. BV is lead coordinator and first investor; QI Tech administers it.
  - Launched the **Cartão Omie** (Mastercard). Its limit is backed by a cash reserve, with no credit analysis.
  - Omie states it has **real-time access to borrowers' revenue and cash flow through the ERP**, which it says gives faster and more precise credit decisions.

### Inferences
- Stone shows the "software as a funnel to banking" thesis did not survive in its Linx form. Management sold the software and returned about R$6bn to shareholders through the dividend plus buybacks. It now relies on payments-to-banking cross-sell. The doubling of the credit book alongside NPL90 rising from 4.67% to 8.60% is a warning: having payments data did not prevent fast credit deterioration.
- Asaas and Cora are the closest analogues to a bank's "operational services" play. Both start from collections (boleto/Pix/billing automation), monetise through fees and float, reach profit, and then layer credit on top (receivables advance). For Cora, credit is now about 20% of the business, and raising ARPU through credit is the stated next lever.
- The ERP/accounting assets (Conta Azul, Omie) get strategic valuations: about R$2bn, and about US$700m for 15%. But their financial-services revenue is still small (Omie about 7%). The value lies in SaaS recurrence plus the accountant channel. Omie's FIDC design, with Omie as first-loss holder, shows that ERP data is being used to underwrite receivables. No default-performance data is disclosed yet.

## Q2. Mexico: Konfío, Clip, Clara, Kapital, Xepelin (MX/CL)

### Takeaway
Mexico's SME fintechs have moved toward **bank licences and profitability over IPOs**:
- **Konfío** (profitable "in the last two years", bank licence pending since 2023) underwrites from 3 months of SAT e-invoice data.
- **Clip** (acquiring) reached profitability in 2025 and now sells software (Wansoft), accounts and credit.
- **Kapital** grew by buying distressed bank assets (Intercam, Sept-2025) and reports ROE of 33%.
- **Xepelin**, a receivables/factoring player, took a bridge round in Jan-2026 at a valuation about 40% lower.
- **Clara** (corporate cards + payables) reached monthly breakeven in Brazil at end-2024 and was near breakeven in Mexico.

  - **About 80,000 SMEs financed** in roughly a decade, and **about MXN 100,000m of credit offered**.
  - More than 80% of clients were taking their first business loan.
  - Target: **85,000 more loans by 2028, with more than MXN 44,000m**. Expects to double credit volume in two years.
  - Products: working-capital loans, **business credit cards, payment terminals, admin tools**.
  - "Crossed the profitability threshold in the last two years"; no figures disclosed.

#### Xepelin. SME receivables finance/factoring + supplier payments (Chile, Mexico)
- From Diario Financiero (3-Jan-2026) [anon. sources]: [DF](https://www.df.cl/df-mas/por-dentro/xepelin-levanta-us-20-millones-en-ronda-liderada-por-nazca-ventures-con)
  - **US$20m bridge round led by Nazca**, at a **valuation down a bit over 40%, from about US$720m to about US$400m**.
  - Revenue ("facturación") of **about US$100m** according to one source. Profitability not disclosed.
  - More than 70k companies served since founding. About 500 staff. Sebastián Kreis is the only founder still in the business.
  - Funding: **Series A July-2021 of US$230m (US$30m equity + US$200m credit facilities)**, Series B of about US$111m (Avenir, Kaszek), and a **US$140m Goldman Sachs line**.
  - Applying for a **Sofipo licence** in Mexico, ambition to be "the Nubank of SMEs". The application was delayed by about 9 months because of the June-2025 US Treasury alert on CIBanco, Intercam and Vector.

### Inferences
- The Mexican winners (Konfío, Clip, Kapital) all aim at **becoming or owning a regulated deposit-taker**. Wholesale-funded credit fintechs face a funding-cost ceiling. Konfío wants SME deposits once licensed.
- Konfío's use of SAT CFDI e-invoice data is the clearest LatAm example of **tax and e-invoice data substituting for collateral** in underwriting. For a bank with e-invoicing or ERP touchpoints this is a directly replicable asset.
- Xepelin's 40% down-round despite about US$100m revenue suggests factoring-led models with wholesale debt were penalised by investors. The reasons are the funding cost and the regulatory shock of 2025.

### Gaps
- Clip merchant count, TPV and revenue are not disclosed in opened sources. A search snippet said "more than 1 million merchants, one in three digital-accepting merchants" and a valuation of more than US$2bn after a 2024 round. Page not opened; **unverified**.
- The El CEO extraction also mentioned a "US$250m late-2016 round led by General Atlantic". This appears to conflate rounds (Clip's widely reported US$250m round was in 2021). **Do not use without verification.**
- Konfío profit amounts, loan book and NPLs: not disclosed. Fate of "Konfío Pagos/ERP": not found.
- Clara revenue: only third-party estimates, which conflict (about US$44m vs a US$100-500m band). Not used.
- Kapital "Tribal" acquisition: no evidence found; not confirmed.
- **Not researched (budget):** Stori Negocios, Fondeadora, Contpaqi/Aspel standalone figures (Aspel is part of Siigo, see Q3), Facturama, Mercado Pago Mexico merchant specifics.

## Q3. Chile, Colombia, Peru, Argentina: Siigo, Bsale, Haulmer/Tuu, Bold, Treinta, Tiendanube/Nuvemshop, Alegra, Nubox, Ualá

### Takeaway
In the Spanish-speaking Andean and Southern Cone region, **accounting and e-invoicing SaaS is consolidating** around Siigo (Accel-KKR), which has about 1.2-1.3m clients and has bought Aspel, Contífico, Memory, Kame and, in Oct-2026, Bsale. **E-invoicing combined with POS** can be profitable without venture capital: Haulmer/Tuu has been profitable since 2020, projected US$35m revenue for 2025 and has bought a prepaid licence. **Acquirers are layering credit and accounts**: Bold disbursed about COP 400bn in sales-based credit in roughly 10 months. **Free digital ledgers failed**: Treinta reached 350 staff with no revenue, then shut its marketplace and went back to paid SaaS with about 30k payers. Tiendanube shows **e-commerce SaaS combined with its own payments**, with 60% of GMV going through NuvemPago.

#### Treinta (Colombia). Free digital ledger → B2B marketplace → back to paid SaaS (failure and pivot case)
- From Growth Talent (11-Apr-2026), founder interview [mgmt claim]: [Growth Talent](https://www.growthtalent.org/insights/hernan-corral-de-pomelo-de-350-empleados-y-63m-a-cerrar-su-apuesta)

### Inferences
- Siigo is the de facto regional consolidator of SME accounting and e-invoicing in Spanish-speaking LatAm, with 1.2-1.3m clients. Any bank strategy based on "owning SME accounting" in Colombia, Mexico or Chile competes with, or could partner with, Siigo/Aspel. No financial-services layer for Siigo was found in the opened sources, which suggests the embedded-finance layer is open for partners.
- Haulmer is the best LatAm evidence that **mandatory e-invoicing plus POS** can be a profitable, capital-light wedge into SMEs, which it is now extending into accounts through a prepaid licence.
- Treinta, together with Kippa and Lummo (Q4/Q5), shows that free ledger apps had poor retention and no monetisation path. The surviving form is a **paid** management tool for a narrower set of businesses.

## Q6. Lessons: which models monetised vs struggled; does accounting/ledger data improve underwriting?

### Takeaway
Across all four regions, **profit sits with whoever controls the money flow**: acquiring, collections, the account and float. Profitable or self-funding examples: Stone, PagBank, Mercado Pago, Asaas, Cora, Clip, Haulmer, Paytm, BharatPe, Moniepoint. **Free bookkeeping and ledger apps failed to monetise**: Treinta pivoted, Kippa is dead, Lummo was liquidated, and Vyapar, OkCredit and Khatabook remain loss-making with small revenue. **Paid ERP/accounting SaaS sold through accountants** is valuable (Conta Azul about R$2bn exit; Omie about US$700m; Siigo's roll-up), but its finance layer is still small. Data-driven underwriting from invoices, sales or ERP data is widespread, with Konfío, Omie, Bold and Cora as examples. However, **no company published hard evidence** such as default rates or Gini comparisons showing that ledger or accounting data beats traditional scoring. Stone's NPL spike is a counter-signal.

### Cited Findings
- **Payments/acquiring + account + credit is profitable at scale:**
  - StoneCo 2Q26 adjusted net income R$582.7m; deposits growth attributed to payments-banking cross-sell. [StoneCo 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001745431/000207097926000275/stoneco_earningsrelease2q2.htm)
  - PagBank 3Q25 recurring net income R$571m; SME working-capital loans +116%. [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/PAGS/pag-bank-registers-a-recurrent-profit-of-brl-571-million-14-growth-u0hfr1hprzk0.html)
  - Clip profitable in 2025. [El CEO](https://elceo.com/negocios/clip-terminales-de-pago-reto-de-ser-unicornio/)
  - Paytm Q1 FY27 PAT ₹220cr; distribution revenue +45%. [Paytm IR](https://paytm.com/blog/investor-relations/paytm-q1-fy27-results-revenue-grows-28-to-2448-crore-highest-ever-quarterly-ebitda-at-203-crore/)
  - BharatPe FY25 EBITDA-positive, about 90% of revenue merchant-led. [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/bharatpe-turns-ebitda-profitable-in-fy25-revenue-touches-rs-1667-cr-10501335); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/how-bharatpe-went-from-inr-5000-cr-loss-to-break-even/)
- **Collections-first SME accounts can be profitable:**

  - Cora profitable since Oct-2024 with a cost to serve of about R$14 per client. [Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/cora-atinge-lucro-projeta-expansao-credito-pme/); [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/startups/cora-apoiada-por-fundos-dos-eua-entra-em-maquininhas-apos-crescer-em-contas-pj/)

  - Vyapar: ₹69cr revenue vs ₹63cr loss. [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/vyapar-posts-rs-63-cr-loss-in-fy25-cash-reserve-fades-93-10819211)

- **The SME neobank without its own balance sheet struggled:** Open (India) lost ₹108.8cr on ₹46cr revenue and has ₹1,921cr of accumulated losses. [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/neobank-unicorn-open-posts-rs-46-cr-revenue-in-fy25-outstanding-losses-mounts-to-rs-1921-cr-10544846)

  - Omie valued at about US$700m, with financial services about 7% of revenue. [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/startups/partners-group-adquire-15-da-omie-por-us-100-milhoes-e-mira-digitalizacao-de-pmes/)
  - Siigo has 1.2-1.3m clients and is buying Bsale. [Forbes Colombia](https://forbes.co/2026/10/07/negocios/siigo-adquiere-la-firma-chilena-bsale-y-anuncia-inversiones-por-us100-millones-en-latinoamerica/)

  - Konfío uses 3 months of SAT invoices instead of about 2 years of history plus collateral. [El Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/mexico/finanzas-economia/el-unicornio-mexicano-konfio-proyecta-duplicar-en-solo-dos-anos-los-creditos-otorgados-en-una-decada)
  - Omie uses real-time ERP revenue and cash flow, with an FIDC in which Omie holds the first-loss junior quota. [Jornal Contábil](https://jornalcontabil.com.br/noticia/omie-expande-atuacao-em-servicos-financeiros-com-fidc-e-cartao-corporativo-para-pmes/)
  - Bold lends against sales and collects through sales discounts. [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tecnologia/fintech-bold-ha-desembolsado-cerca-de-400-000-millones-en-creditos-a-pymes-que-hoy-se-financian-para-crecer-491054)
  - Cora uses transaction data to assess risk. [Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/cora-atinge-lucro-projeta-expansao-credito-pme/)
- **Counter-signal:** StoneCo's NPL>90d rose from 4.67% (2Q25) to 8.60% (2Q26) while its credit book grew 107.5%. [StoneCo 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001745431/000207097926000275/stoneco_earningsrelease2q2.htm)

### Inferences
- For a bank, the evidence points to these priorities:
  1. **Own or partner for the collections and acquiring flow.** Boleto/Pix-type collections, payment links, POS and tap-to-phone generate fees, float and the best credit data. Asaas, Cora, Clip, Bold and Haulmer all built credit on top of collections or acquiring.
  2. **Partner with accounting/ERP and e-invoicing SaaS** (Siigo/Aspel, Omie, Conta Azul/Visma, Alegra, Nubox) rather than building free bookkeeping. Accountants are the distribution channel: 28k offices for Omie, a rising share of Cora's acquisition, 7k distributors for Siigo.
  3. **Do not expect a free ledger app to monetise on its own.** Every free-ledger case in this sample needed a paid tier, a lending layer or a pivot, and several failed.
- Two models, two cost bases:
  - The ERP and e-invoicing players are monetising finance through **receivables advance and secured cards** (Omie's FIDC; Omie's card secured by a cash reserve). These are low-risk entry products.
  - The acquirers move faster into **working capital**, with higher NPL risk (Stone).
- Cost to serve matters. Cora's figure of about R$14 per client, and its claim that this is about 10% of incumbents' cost, is a benchmark a bank's SME digital account must approach to compete on micro-SMEs.

### Gaps
- No public study or company disclosure was found that quantifies the predictive lift from accounting or ledger data, such as default rates of ledger-data borrowers vs others. All underwriting claims are qualitative company statements.
- Unit economics such as CAC, LTV and ARPU are mostly undisclosed. Exceptions are Cora's cost to serve and ARPU target, and Vyapar's ₹2.04 spent per ₹1 of revenue.
- The search budget was exhausted before the following candidates could be checked: Inter Empresas, Cielo, Nibo, iugu, Vindi, Cobre Fácil, Stori, Fondeadora, Contpaqi, Facturama, Fintoc, Tenpo, Nequi Negocios, Culqi/Yape, Naranja X, Defontana, Nubox (financials), Alegra (financials), Instamojo, PhonePe, Funding Societies, Moka/GoTo, BukuWarung (P&L), Paystack, Flutterwave, OPay, TymeBank, Lipa Later, Duka, Sendy. A follow-up pass is needed for these.
