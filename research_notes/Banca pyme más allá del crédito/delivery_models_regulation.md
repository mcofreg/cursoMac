## 1. Operating models: build vs partner vs buy vs white-label (bank cases, costs, timelines, outcomes, consultant frameworks)

### Takeaway
The documented record of bank acquisitions of SME software is mixed. NatWest–FreeAgent (2018, ~£53m) is still the clearest "buy and give it away with the account" case still running. Société Générale (Shine, bought 2020 for ~€100m, sold 2024) and BBVA (Holvi, bought 2016, sold 2021) both exited. In Shine's case the asset later went into a group that Cegid valued at more than €1bn (Nov 2025). Capital One–Brex (closed Apr 2026, $5.15bn) is the largest recent bank purchase, but Brex is spend management and cards rather than generic SME back-office software. Consultants favour building only on the core banking platform and partnering for adjacent tools. They report that most ecosystem plays fail: BCG puts long-run ecosystem sustainability below 15%. McKinsey gives a minimum viable product (MVP) cost of €5–20m and subscription pricing of €6–15 per month.

**Consultant frameworks**
- **McKinsey, "How banks can use ecosystems to win in the SME market" (10 Jun 2019).**
  - Three roles: Participate (partner with at-scale platforms; e.g. GoBank on Uber), Orchestrate (e.g. Idea Bank Poland's SME cloud platform, which included a bookkeeping app for Uber drivers in 2017) and Build (e.g. OTP eBIZ, RBC Ownr).
  - Some banks spent €5–20m on their MVP.
  - Subscription bundles: one European bank charges €6–15 per month, usually with about 3-month free trials. Other revenue: commissions on third-party services, data use, lead generation and listing fees.
  - SMEs are about one-fifth of global banking revenue (about $850bn).
  - Failure causes: complexity without monetisation; housing the initiative in the SME product team (lacks start-up focus, top-management attention and skills); legacy-IT entanglement blocking a spin-off; launch delays from integrating too deeply into the existing app.
  - Source: [McKinsey (karriere.mckinsey.de mirror) [opened]](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)
- **Bain, "Small Business Ecosystems: Banks' Next Challenge" (19 Mar 2018).**
  - Three approaches: segment-focused propositions (mostly in-house), life-event ecosystems (usually with partners) and complete solutions to a recurring pain point (payments plus analytics).
  - Advice: build on strong existing platforms; partner for adjacent services (e.g. POS terminals); stay close to core banking where the bank has credibility.
  - Monetisation options: referral fees, commissions or revenue share, or free services in exchange for retention and data. Ancillary profits may be smaller than core profits.
  - Source: [Bain [opened]](https://bain.com/insights/small-business-ecosystems-banks-next-challenge)
- **BCG Henderson Institute (22 Jun 2020).**
  - Studied 100+ failed ecosystems; fewer than 15% were sustainable long-term (about 85% failure).
  - Causes of failure: governance (over one-third), configuration 18%, execution 15%, insufficient problem 10%, weak defensibility 10%, launch 8%, monetisation 5%.
  - 7 in 10 failures happened after reaching the scale phase.
  - Financial-services case: Bankcard (Australia/NZ bank JV, 1974–2006), lost out to Visa and Mastercard.
  - Source: [BCG [opened]](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2020/why-do-most-business-ecosystems-fail)
- **BCG adoption statistic.** Only 27% of top banks had engaged in ecosystems to a significant extent. — [cited in search summary, original BCG page not opened [snippet]](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)

### Inferences
- **Cost ranges, from the evidence:**

  | Path | Cost evidence |
  |---|---|
  | Partner / white-label (Melio, Autobooks) | Low capex; VENDOR CLAIM of weeks to launch |
  | Build (McKinsey MVP) | €5–20m |
  | Buy a niche accounting tool | about £53m (FreeAgent 2018) |
  | Buy an SME neobank | about €100m (Shine 2020) |
  | Buy a scaled SME fintech | $2.5bn (Melio) to $5.15bn (Brex) |

- **Sales timing.** SG sold Shine about 4 years after buying it. Within about 1 year the asset sat inside a group valued at more than €1bn (not like-for-like, since it had been merged with Ageras). This suggests banks may struggle to hold SME-software assets long enough to capture value, especially when CEOs change. Ageras/Shine and Holvi both needed their own licence or independence after the bank exit.
- **The "free with account" bundle** (NatWest/FreeAgent, DNB Regnskap pricing aside) seems to be the dominant monetisation of bank-owned accounting software. The loss reported at FreeAgent hints at cross-subsidy cost, but that rests on a [snippet].
- **White-label providers are consolidating into strategic owners.** Melio is now Xero-owned; Autobooks bought Allied. Banks that white-label AP/AR face change-of-control risk on the vendor (see section 4).
- **US invoicing is still a minority behaviour inside banks (18%)** even where it is offered by banks holding a quarter of relationships. Distribution alone does not ensure adoption.

### Gaps
- No public P&L, adoption or ROI figures for bank-owned SME tools (NatWest has not disclosed FreeAgent penetration among its SME base; Chase invoicing has no published usage).
- Could not verify DNB–Fiken. Treat as likely erroneous unless another source confirms.
- No verified data on HSBC Kinetic, Lloyds, Starling Toolkit, Tide or ING outcomes (Starling page 403; searches exhausted).
- McKinsey's later ecosystem pieces and Oliver Wyman/Accenture frameworks were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- Capital One's Brex integration costs ($950m) are unverified.

## 2. Vendor and partner landscape serving banks for SME tools (named bank clients, published results)

### Takeaway
The US has a mature white-label stack:
- **Autobooks** for invoicing, acceptance, bookkeeping and, since May 2025, bill pay. Clients include TD and Citizens.
- **Melio** for AP/AR, via Capital One, Fiserv CashFlow Central and Clover. Now owned by Xero.
- **BILL**, which relies on banks (JPMorgan) for rails rather than being bank-distributed.
- **Codat** for accounting-data connectivity into commercial banks (BMO, JPMorgan).

Published results are almost all vendor-reported and qualitative. In LatAm, the visible pattern is bank + local e-invoicing/ERP vendor partnerships (Siigo–Davivienda in Colombia) rather than white-label. I found no verified bank partnerships for Nubox, Alegra, Bsale or Defontana.

### Inferences
- **For a LatAm bank:**
  - Local e-invoicing and ERP vendors (Siigo, Alegra, Nubox) are natural partners, because they hold tax-authority-certified invoicing and accountant distribution.
  - Bank-group acquirers can now bundle invoicing in Chile.
  - US-style white-label AP/AR (Melio, Autobooks) has no visible LatAm equivalent in the evidence gathered.
- **Vendor consolidation** (Melio→Xero, Allied→Autobooks, Ageras/Shine→Cegid) means the "neutral white-label supplier" may become owned by a competitor or by an accounting platform.

## 3. Regulation: can a bank legally offer accounting, marketing, e-invoicing or software to SMEs? (Chile, Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Brazil, EU/Spain, UK, USA; data, liability, capital)

### Takeaway
Across jurisdictions, banks can usually offer such tools only:
- if the activity fits a "financial / ancillary / data-processing" perimeter;
- through a separately authorised subsidiary or affiliate; or
- by acting as distributor, referrer or "finder" for a third-party vendor.

Chile is strict and enforced. A bank support company (SAG) was fined in 2024 for enabling e-receipt issuance outside its exclusive purpose (giro exclusivo). Since 2023, Chilean payment-card-operator subsidiaries may issue e-invoices and boletas for merchants. In Mexico, banks may expressly keep accounting for companies (art. 46 fr. XIX LIC). They may also own "generic service companies" that serve third parties, under CNBV authorisation. The US permits tax preparation, consulting with revenue caps, data processing up to 49% non-financial, finder activities and software, with anti-tying limits. In the EU and UK the constraint is mostly prudential: non-financial holdings above 15%/60% of capital get a 1250% risk weight, and software has a capital deduction regime that is full in the UK. Open-finance timelines are slipping: Chile's system starts July 2027 (possibly 2028), and EU FIDA is set for withdrawal (Nov 2026).

### Cited Findings

**Chile**
- **Sociedades de apoyo al giro (SAG; bank support companies).**
  - Banks may hold shares only in companies whose sole purpose is (1) providing services that help financial entities fulfil their purposes, or (2) carrying out certain banking operations with the public, excluding deposit-taking.
  - Examples: electronic fund and data transfer, credit card administration, ATMs, bill collection.
  - Prior CMF authorisation is required, and the CMF supervises them.
  - Source: [CMF Educa, "Sociedades de Apoyo al Giro Bancario" [opened]](https://www.cmfchile.cl/educa/621/w3-propertyvalue-29001.html)
- **Legal basis.** SAGs are governed by the LGB (cited as art. 74) and RAN chapter 11-6. RAN 11-6 has an annex listing authorised services (e.g. collections, financial advisory, promotion/distribution of bank products). CMF Circular 31 (9 Feb 2017) sets the general SAG rules. — [search summary of CMF pages [snippet]](https://www.cmfchile.cl/portal/normativa/624/w4-article-47024.html). The LGB text on bcn.cl would not load, so the article numbers are unverified.
- **Enforcement case: Transbank fine (18 Oct 2024).** The CMF fined Transbank S.A. (a bank-owned SAG) UF 1,000. Together with an external company, Transbank had built a mechanism integrating its systems to let its clients issue electronic sales receipts (boletas). The CMF found this exceeded its exclusive and authorised purpose under the LGB and RAN chapter 11-6 (Res. Exenta 9244). — [CMF press [opened]](https://www.cmfchile.cl/portal/prensa/625/w4-article-86185.html)
- **NCG 498 (11 Oct 2023).** Payment-card operators may offer: (i) sales reporting, reconciliation and cash-register balancing; (ii) issuance of electronic invoices and receipts for transactions with any payment method; (iii) third-party collection.
  - Conditions: the services may be provided directly or outsourced (the operator stays responsible; RAN 20-7 applies), and only to merchants with which the operator has contracts.
  - Bank-owned operators organised as SAGs were previously barred from these activities. They may leave the SAG registry and become bank payment-operator subsidiaries under LGB art. 70 letter b), which lets them perform the activities.
  - Source: [CMF press [opened]](https://www.cmfchile.cl/portal/prensa/625/w4-article-74109.html)
- **Open finance (Ley Fintec 21.521).**
  - NCG 569 (1 Jun 2026) amends NCG 514 and adds Technical Annex 3.
  - The system enters into force 36 months after NCG 514's first publication, which the article places in July 2027.
  - Phasing for banks and card issuers runs from 5 months (terms/channels APIs) to 18 months (payment-initiation APIs for legal entities with multiple mandates, and recurring payments). Other article 18 entities get 20–30 months.
  - Source: [Cuatrecasas, 19 Jun 2026 [opened]](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/latam/servicios-financieros-seguros/art/cmf-ncg-569-reglas-tecnicas-sistema-finanzas-abiertas)
  - Conflict: an Aug 2026 CMF presentation reportedly cites 3 Jul 2028 as the 36-month milestone, with a sandbox from Oct 2026. — [CMF PDF [snippet]](https://www.cmfchile.cl/portal/prensa/625/articles-113925_doc_pdf.pdf)

**Mexico**
- **Art. 46 LIC (29 fractions of permitted operations).**
  - Fraction XIX covers keeping accounting and corporate books for companies, which is an express authority relevant to bookkeeping.
  - Fraction XXIX allows "análogas o conexas" (analogous or related) operations authorised by SHCP after hearing Banxico and CNBV.
  - Last reform shown: DOF 10 Jan 2014. The portal flags that this has not been checked against the official text.
  - Source: [mley.mx LIC art. 46 [opened]](https://mley.mx/LIC/articulo/46/)
- **Art. 88 LIC.** Banks need CNBV authorisation to invest in companies that provide "servicios complementarios o auxiliares" to their administration or business, and in real-estate companies for their offices. These companies are subject to CNBV rules and supervision fees. Last reform: DOF 10 Jan 2014. — [mley.mx LIC art. 88 [opened]](https://mley.mx/LIC/articulo/88/)
- **Art. 89 LIC.** Covers CNBV-authorised investments in foreign financial entities and domestic financial entities. A 51% control threshold triggers compliance duties. — [mley.mx LIC art. 89 [opened]](https://mley.mx/LIC/articulo/89/)
- **SHCP rules for art. 88 companies (DOF 24 Oct 2005, signed 12 Oct 2005).**
  - "Empresas de Servicios Exclusivas" serve only the controlling bank.
  - "Empresas de Servicios Genéricas" may serve banks "sin perjuicio de que podrán prestar servicios a otras personas" (and may also serve other persons). To keep that status they must earn at least 5% of gross annual revenue from services to banks.
  - Revocation leads to a 180-day divestment period.
  - Source: [SHCP Reglas, hosted by ABM [opened, PDF text extracted]](https://www.abm.org.mx/legislacion-y-regulacion/juridico/shcp/Reglasgeneralesalasquehabrandesujetarse%20lassociedadesaqueserefiereelarticulo88delaLIC.pdf)
  - Caveat: the 2014 reform gave rule-making to the CNBV. Whether these 2005 SHCP rules have been replaced by CNBV rules was not verified.

  - Excluded: financial-sector entities and undertakings the authority considers a direct extension of, or ancillary to, banking.

**USA**
- **Regulation Y (bank holding companies).**
  - 12 CFR 225.28(b)(6)(vi) permits "Providing tax-planning and tax-preparation services to any person."
  - (b)(9) permits management consulting to other companies on financial, economic, accounting or audit matters. Other consulting is capped at 30% of the company's consulting revenue.
  - (b)(14) permits data processing, storage and transmission of financial, banking or economic data. Other data processing is allowed if it does not exceed 49% of data-processing revenue.
  - No explicit bookkeeping or payroll item appeared in the fetched text.
  - Source: [12 CFR 225.28 (Cornell LII) [opened]](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/225.28)
- **OCC 12 CFR 7.5006 (national banks).** Data processing is part of the business of banking. Other data is allowed where convenient or useful, but revenue must be derived "predominantly" from financial, banking and economic data. - **OCC 12 CFR 7.1002 (finder).** A national bank may identify parties, introduce them, forward applications, arrange discounts from third-party providers and do record-keeping, including as an electronic finder. It may not engage in unapproved brokerage. This is the legal basis for referral and marketplace models. — [12 CFR 7.1002 [opened]](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/7.1002)
- **Anti-tying, 12 USC 1972(1).** Banks may not condition credit, property or services on the customer obtaining other products, except a "loan, discount, deposit, or trust service". The Fed may grant exceptions. — [12 USC 1972 [opened]](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/1972)

### Inferences
- **Common structures by jurisdiction:**
  - Chile: (a) distribution or referral partnership with a third-party vendor (no SAG needed); (b) an art. 70 b) payment-operator subsidiary for merchant e-invoicing and reconciliation since NCG 498; (c) minority investment. Generic accounting or marketing software inside a SAG is high-risk given the Transbank precedent.
  - Mexico: direct bank service under art. 46 fr. XIX (accounting) or XXIX (SHCP-authorised "conexas"); or a CNBV-authorised "Empresa de Servicios Genérica" that can serve third parties.
  - US: a bank holding company (BHC) or financial holding company (FHC) affiliate under Reg Y, or a bank-level data-processing, software or finder authority.
  - EU/UK: subsidiary or minority stake, sized against CRR art. 89 limits and software deductions.
- **Free-software-with-account bundles** (the NatWest/FreeAgent pattern) look compatible with US anti-tying when the required product is a deposit account (a traditional product). Conditioning credit on buying non-traditional software would be problematic. This needs counsel confirmation.
- **E-invoicing mandates create a regulated product.** Spain (Verifactu/B2B, now 2027–2028), the EU (ViDA) and UK MTD (2026–2028) give banks a reason to offer certified invoicing and tax tools. They also expose banks to software-certification and liability obligations (Verifactu software-producer duties remain).

## 4. Risks: reputational/advice errors, data security, vendor concentration, fee cannibalisation, accountant-channel conflict, antitrust/tying

### Takeaway
The best-documented risks are:
- execution and governance failure (BCG: about 85% of ecosystems fail; McKinsey: complexity without monetisation);
- regulatory-perimeter breaches (Chile's Transbank fine);
- partner non-delivery damaging bank credibility (Bain);
- vendor change of control (Melio→Xero; Shine→Ageras→Cegid);
- cross-subsidy cost of "free with account" bundles (FreeAgent losses, [snippet]).

Accountants are a channel to court, not to bypass: FreeAgent gives free licences to accountancy practices of NatWest clients, and Cegid/Shine target 15,000 accountants. Tying rules (US 12 USC 1972) constrain how bundles are conditioned.

### Cited Findings
- **Bain risks:**
  - Partners may not deliver, damaging the bank's credibility as organiser.
  - Banks have low credibility for non-bank services.
  - Choice between exclusive and open-architecture partnerships.
  - Unclear customer-support ownership; staff need knowledge of ancillary services.
  - Ownership of data and customer contact.
  - Uncertain monetisation; ancillary profits may be smaller than core.
  - Source: [Bain 2018 [opened]](https://bain.com/insights/small-business-ecosystems-banks-next-challenge)
- **McKinsey risks.** Complexity without material benefit; weak internal ownership; legacy-IT entanglement; integration delays; data monetisation constrained by GDPR. — [McKinsey 2019 [opened]](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-banks-can-use-ecosystems-to-win-in-the-sme-market)
- **BCG failure analysis.** Fewer than 15% of ecosystems were sustainable. Governance errors caused over one-third of failures, and design flaws about 85%. — [BCG 2020 [opened]](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2020/why-do-most-business-ecosystems-fail)
- **Regulatory-perimeter risk.** CMF fined Transbank UF 1,000 for an e-receipt integration outside its exclusive purpose (Oct 2024). — [CMF [opened]](https://www.cmfchile.cl/portal/prensa/625/w4-article-86185.html)
- **Anti-tying.** 12 USC 1972 bans conditioning bank services on non-traditional products. — [Cornell LII [opened]](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/1972)
- **Vendor change of control.** Melio, white-label AP for Capital One, Fiserv and Clover, is now owned by Xero. — [Melio partners [opened]](https://melio.com/partners/); [CPA Practice Advisor [opened]](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/10/15/xero-acquires-payments-platform-melio-for-2-5b/170967/)
- **Dependency on bank rails.** BILL depends on bank partnerships as a money transmitter. — [American Banker [opened]](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/what-bill-holdings-gets-from-the-jpmorgan-wallet)
- **Cross-subsidy and fee cannibalisation.** FreeAgent's losses were partly attributed to free provision with NatWest accounts. — [AccountingWeb [snippet]](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/226521)
- **Accountant channel.** FreeAgent markets free NatWest/RBS/Ulster/Mettle-linked software to accountancy practices ("hundreds of potential new clients"). — [FreeAgent accountants page [snippet]](https://www.freeagent.com/accountants/try/natwest-rbs-free-licences/)
- **Accountant-centred SME finance hubs.** Cegid/Shine combine 1m+ SMEs and 15,000 accountants. — [Maddyness [opened]](https://www.maddyness.com/2025/11/26/shine-18-mois-apres-son-rachat-par-ageras-la-fintech-bascule-dans-le-giron-de-cegid/)
- **Low adoption risk.** Only 18% of US small businesses send invoices through their bank, even after major banks launched invoicing. — [Autobooks/Javelin [opened]](https://blog.autobooks.co/small-businesses-want-invoicing-where-they-already-bank)

### Inferences
- **Buy** carries holding-period and CEO-change risk (SG/Shine, BBVA/Holvi exits).
- **White-label** carries vendor change-of-control and roadmap risk (Melio).
- **Build** carries the €5–20m MVP cost and IT-entanglement risk.
- **Partner/referral** carries credibility risk when the partner fails, but it is the lowest regulatory-perimeter risk in strict regimes such as Chile.
- **Tax and bookkeeping errors** (e.g., FreeAgent/Mettle tax estimates, Verifactu compliance) would become bank-branded errors. No liability allocation evidence was found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No documented incidents of advice or tax errors, data breaches or antitrust actions tied to bank SME-software bundles were found.
- No EU or LatAm competition-law guidance on bank bundling of SME software was found.
- No quantified fee-cannibalisation data, e.g. lost cash-management or acquiring fees.
