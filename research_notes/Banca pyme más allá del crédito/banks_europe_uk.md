# European and UK banks offering SMEs non-lending operational services: case files and reported results (as of Nov 2026)

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[AUDITED/REPORTED]**: annual report, statutory accounts or regulated results release.
- **[COMPANY CLAIM]**: bank or fintech press release or marketing page, not audited.
- **[PRESS]**: secondary press report.
- **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**: seen only in a search-engine snippet. The page was not opened, so the figure is unverified. These appear only in the Gaps sections.

Research note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Several candidate banks were never searched: BNP Paribas, Nordea, Danske, Swedbank, Handelsbanken, Sparkassen, Commerzbank, Intesa, UniCredit, Bankinter, Kutxabank, Unicaja, Belfius and Santander UK. Their gaps are listed at the end. 21 cases are covered below, with depth on NatWest, Starling, Monzo, SpareBank 1 Regnskapshuset, Société Générale/Shine, CaixaBank, ABN AMRO/Tikkie and ING/Payvision.

## Q1. UK: what did banks offer SMEs beyond lending, how, at what price, and with what reported results?

### Takeaway
NatWest has the most complete UK disclosure. Its 2025 annual report sizes three non-lending SME units: FreeAgent accounting (~225k customers), Mettle digital account (~150k open accounts) and Tyl acquiring (~40k merchants). Growth is steady but modest, and Mettle is far below the 500k-customers-in-five-years ambition stated in 2022. The licensed challengers (Starling, Monzo) show the clearest monetisation of SME "toolkits" through subscriptions. Monzo reports 47% of monthly-active business customers paying and Business at 14% of group revenue. Starling reports 56% SME primacy. Incumbents are moving to free, embedded tools (Lloyds "Get Paid" with BankiFi, 2026) or exiting capital-heavy acquiring (Barclays–Brookfield, 2025).

### Cited Findings

#### Case 1: NatWest Group (FreeAgent, Mettle, Tyl, Payit/Prommt)
- **What and how:**
  - FreeAgent cloud accounting was **acquired** in 2018 for about £53m (Wikipedia, citing AccountingWEB 27 Mar 2018) — [Wikipedia: FreeAgent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeAgent)
  - Mettle is a **built** app-only business account for sole traders and small limited companies. It lets customers "raise and send invoices, upload receipts and integrate with FreeAgent". Mettle+ was expanded in 2025 with quotes that convert to invoices — [NatWest Group Annual Report & Accounts 2025, published 13 Feb 2026](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
  - Tyl is NatWest's **own** merchant acquiring brand. In 2025 it launched "a new suite of terminals and a merchant portal" — [NatWest ARA 2025](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
  - In Aug 2025 NatWest **partnered** with Prommt, a pay-by-link platform that "combines our business payment solutions Payit and Tyl into a single seamless solution" (open banking plus card, branded payment requests, recurring payments) — [NatWest ARA 2025](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
- **Pricing:**
  - FreeAgent is "available for free* to anyone with a NatWest, Royal Bank of Scotland or Ulster Bank business current account". The footnote reads "terms and conditions apply; optional add-ons may be chargeable". FreeAgent's in-app tax calculation was extended to all NatWest Group business customers on 23 Mar 2024, after launching first for Mettle customers in late 2023 — [FreeAgent press release, 23 Mar 2024](https://www.freeagent.com/en/company/press-room/freeagent-natwest-tax-calculation/) [COMPANY CLAIM]
- **Reported results, 31 Dec 2025 vs 2024** — all from [NatWest ARA 2025](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]:
  - Mettle: "approximately 150,000 open accounts, up from approximately 132,000 at the end of 2024" (about +14%).
  - FreeAgent: "approximately 225,000 customers in 2025, compared with 200,000 in 2024" (about +12.5%).
  - Tyl: "supported approximately 40,000 merchants during 2025, compared with approximately 38,000 in 2024" (about +5%).
  - Mettle "debuted strongly" in the CMA service-quality survey, "ranking second overall".
- **Definitional conflict:** FreeAgent described its own base in Mar 2024 as "over 250,000 small businesses, accountants and bookkeepers" — [FreeAgent PR, 23 Mar 2024](https://www.freeagent.com/en/company/press-room/freeagent-natwest-tax-calculation/) [COMPANY CLAIM]. NatWest counts 200k customers for 2024. The FreeAgent figure probably includes accountants and practice clients.
- **Mettle's original ambition and cost** — all from [Euromoney, 14 Nov 2022](https://www.euromoney.com/article/2avw0irwnarql0nxf708w/fintech/natwest-spins-mettle-tech-into-banking-as-a-service-venture/) [PRESS]:
  - In Nov 2022 Mettle was "on track to roughly triple its direct customers in 2022, reaching 100,000". It "could reach as many as 500,000 direct customers within five years".
  - NatWest moved Mettle's core tech and staff into a UK banking-as-a-service JV with Vodeno (Warburg Pincus-backed). The commitment was a "capped commitment of £115 million", and NatWest invested "€58 million in Vodeno".
  - The JV was "aiming to break even within five years". Mettle's brand, customers and revenues stayed with NatWest.
- **Profitability:** the 2025 annual report breaks out no revenue or profit for Mettle, FreeAgent or Tyl. FreeAgent Central Ltd, FreeAgent Holdings Ltd and Mettle Ventures Ltd appear as subsidiaries in the report's entity list — [NatWest ARA 2025](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf)

#### Case 2: Starling Bank (bank-licensed; Business Toolkit / SME subscriptions, Ember accounting, Engine SaaS)
- **Strategy:** the FY25 annual report strategy chart lists "Chargeable SME products to increase fee income", with "SME subscriptions" as a fee lever — [Starling Annual Report 2025 (FY to 31 Mar 2025)](https://www.starlingbank.com/docs/annual-reports/Starling-Bank-Annual-Report-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
- **FY25 fee data** — all from [Starling AR 2025](https://www.starlingbank.com/docs/annual-reports/Starling-Bank-Annual-Report-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]:
  - "Other fee income" (marketplace, FX, Fleet and subscription fees, i.e. fees and commissions less card and SaaS income) was **£18.058m** in FY25, against £16.868m in FY24. That is about £6 per core account and caps what SME subscriptions can be contributing.
  - Total fees and commissions were £128.3m, of which card transaction revenue was £101.5m.
  - Group revenue was £714m (FY24: £682m).
  - SME deposits were **£5.7bn** (2024: £6.0bn) and SME card spend £4.4bn. Both "dipped" as "entrepreneurs grappled with a challenging operating environment".
  - Engine (SaaS) fee income was £8.7m, up 284%.
- **FY26, year to 31 Mar 2026** — all from [Starling press release, 21 May 2026](https://www.starlingbank.com/news/starling-delivers-fifth-year-of-profitability-and-accelerates-global-growth-strategy) [COMPANY CLAIM, results release]:
  - Revenue was £887.4m and PBT £217.1m. The release states FY25 revenue as £940.0m, which conflicts with the £714m in the FY25 annual report. This is probably a change in revenue definition; unresolved.
  - **SME primacy is 56% vs 35% for retail.** Primacy means crediting at least £500 a month on average.
  - SME account openings "more than tripled" in Apr 2026 vs Apr 2025.
  - Starling **acquired Ember**, an all-in-one SME accounting and tax platform (price undisclosed). Within six months it launched an HMRC-recognised Making Tax Digital solution for sole traders and landlords. No adoption figures were given.
  - Engine revenue grew about 25%, with £70m committed ARR.
- No Business Toolkit subscriber count or Toolkit revenue is disclosed in either document.

#### Case 3: Monzo Business (bank-licensed; paid tiers Lite/Pro/Team)
- **FY25 (12 months to Mar 2025)** — [tech.eu, 2 Jun 2025](https://tech.eu/2025/06/02/monzo-reveals-one-third-of-customers-use-it-as-a-primary-bank-as-profits-and-revenues-swell/) [PRESS, reporting the annual report]:
  - Business customers grew 49% to **625,000**. Monzo Business made up **12% of group revenue**, and business ARPU rose 16%.
  - Group: revenue £1.2bn, PBT £60.5m, subscription income £75.2m (+50%, group-wide, not split by segment).
- Monzo was "Britain's most recommended business account" for overall service quality in the BVA BDRC surveys for 2023 and 2024 — [Monzo Annual Report 2025 page](https://monzo.com/annual-report/2025) [COMPANY CLAIM, citing an independent survey]
- **FY26 (reported Aug 2026)** — [Dealroom news, 17 Aug 2026, citing Tech.eu 13 Aug 2026](https://dealroom.co/news/145245-monzo-business-reaches-one-million-customers/) [PRESS]:
  - **1 million** Monzo Business customers, "one in six UK businesses".
  - Business contributed **14% of the group's £1.7bn revenue**, up from 12%. That implies about £238m, but this is my calculation, not a reported figure.
  - **"47% of monthly active business customers were paid subscribers."** Pricing: Lite free, Pro £9 a month, Team £25 a month.
  - Revenue sources are subscriptions, interchange, interest on deposits and lending. No per-tool adoption (invoicing, tax pots) is disclosed.

#### Case 4: Lloyds Banking Group ("Get Paid" invoicing with BankiFi)
- **Launched:** announced 29 Sep 2026, "will roll out later this year to UK business customers" — [TechRadar Pro, 29 Sep 2026](https://techradar.com/pro/lloyds-new-in-app-invoicing-tool-wants-to-help-uk-small-businesses-cut-their-admin-for-good) [PRESS / COMPANY CLAIM]
- **Offer:** a "Get Paid" invoicing and payment-collection tool inside the Lloyds app and online banking. Customers can create and send invoices, issue secure payment links, track paid and outstanding requests, and use automated reminders.
- **How:** **partnered**, "powered by Lloyds' strategic partnership with BankiFi".
- **Price:** "at no additional cost and without the need to purchase separate invoicing or accountancy software".
- Lloyds frames it as part of "embedded accounting". No results yet.

#### Case 5: Barclays (Barclaycard Payments: partial exit from acquiring)
- **Deal:** announced 17 Apr 2025, a partnership with Brookfield to turn the payment acceptance (merchant acquiring) business into a standalone entity — [PYMNTS, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2025/barclays-sell-stake-payment-acceptance-business-brookfield/) [PRESS, from the press release]
  - Barclays invests about **£400m**, mostly in years 1–3.
  - Brookfield may buy about 70% between years 3 and 7, and its incentive converts into a further 10%, taking it to about 80%. Barclays would retain about 20%.
  - The business stays "the sole payment acceptance provider to Barclays' clients for at least 10 years" under the Barclaycard Payments brand.
  - Rationale: "simpler, better and more balanced bank".

#### Case 6: HSBC UK (HSBC Kinetic mobile SME bank)
- HSBC UK fully launched Kinetic on 24 Mar 2021. "HSBC Kinetic lends over £2M since becoming first digital bank to offer small business loans" (22 Jul 2022). An international payments tool was added on 17 Jun 2024, and carbon-insight tools for small business customers in Mar/May 2024 — [HSBC UK newsroom](https://www.about.hsbc.co.uk/news-and-media/hsbc-kinetic-lends-over-euro-2m-since-becoming-first-digital-bank-to-offer-small-business-loans) [COMPANY CLAIM]
- No customer numbers were found. **The newsroom shows Kinetic still active as of mid-2024; no closure or fold-back announcement was found.**

### Inferences
- NatWest's three units grew only 5–14% in 2025. Mettle at about 150k accounts by end-2025 is far from the 2022 ambition of up to 500k within five years. Bank-built digital SME brands appear to scale much more slowly than licensed challengers: Monzo Business added about 375k customers in FY26 (625k to 1m).
- Bundling free accounting software (NatWest/FreeAgent) is a retention and engagement play, not a revenue line. NatWest never discloses FreeAgent P&L, and most of its about 225k customers likely pay nothing as bank customers. This is an inference from the free-for-customers terms.
- Monzo and Starling show that SMEs will pay for bundled admin tools when they are packaged as account tiers: 47% paid penetration among active Monzo Business users. Monzo Business revenue share rose from 12% to 14% in a year.
- Incumbent acquiring is being restructured rather than expanded. Barclays is moving to a minority position, and NatWest's Tyl is growing about 5% a year while partnering with Prommt for pay-by-link.

  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] 50,000 customers and a 600% rise in deposits (Finextra, Mar 2022); the page returned 403.
  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] Mettle's FreeAgent offer requires at least one transaction a month (Finder/review sites).
- **FreeAgent:**
  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] Endole shows FreeAgent Central Ltd turnover band under £54m (FY2024).
  - Actual revenue and profit need the Companies House filing (SC316774), which was not retrieved.
- **Tyl:** launch date (2019 per Finextra, [SNIPPET-ONLY]) and processing volumes not found.
- **Starling Business Toolkit:**
  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] priced at £7 a month (the Toolkit page returned 403).
  - No subscriber or revenue figure was found in FY25 or FY26 materials.
- **Barclays:** [SNIPPET-ONLY] PaymentsJournal (Mar 2025) reported a ~£650m valuation vs ~£2bn hoped for. Completion status in 2026 is unverified.
- **Lloyds:** [SNIPPET-ONLY] BankiFi Making Tax Digital tool on 19 May 2026. "Cashflow insight" as a product name was not confirmed.
- **Not researched:** Santander UK, and Barclays' Xero/FreeAgent partnerships (no searches left).

## Q2. Spain: CaixaBank, BBVA, Santander, Sabadell: non-lending SME services and results

### Takeaway
Spanish banks lead on acquiring and POS bundling, but disclose almost no results specific to non-lending tools. CaixaBank (31.3% share of the merchant segment, over 1.5m self-employed clients) bundles POS fee rebates, Facilitea discounts and partner invoicing software. Its partner is Quipu for Verifactu, a **paid** €9.56–€19.96/month subscription that CaixaBank only "facilitates access" to. BBVA's documented SME moves are mostly credit-led: invoice financing embedded in the SERES e-invoicing platform, and a multi-purpose credit line. Santander's main non-lending SME bet is Ebury, which it is partially deconsolidating in 2026.

### Cited Findings

#### Case 7: CaixaBank (Negocios / autónomos, TPV through Comercia Global Payments, Quipu for Verifactu, Facilitea)
- **Scale and offer** — [CaixaBank news, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.caixabank.com/es/actualidad/noticias/caixabank-refuerza-su-estrategia-en-autonomos-con-beneficios-vinculados-a-la-actividad-del-negocio) [COMPANY CLAIM]
  - "Más de 1,5 millones de autónomos trabajan con CaixaBank, más del 43% de los que operan en España". CaixaBank reaches "una cuota del 31,3% en el segmento de comercios".
  - Network: 68 specialised offices for the self-employed and 2,500 managers.
  - **Bundling:** a TPV (POS) fee rebate for 12 months for new merchants and self-employed customers billing at least €2,000 a month.
  - Incentives for direct-debiting the self-employed social-security payment, with **Facilitea discounts** or free POS services.
  - MyCommerce Smartphone TPV (contactless acceptance on the phone).
  - No results of the campaign were disclosed.
- **E-invoicing / Verifactu** — [CaixaBank Verifactu page, accessed Nov 2026](https://www.caixabank.es/empresa/bancadistancia/verifactu_es.html) [COMPANY CLAIM]
  - **Partner** model: Quipu (Quipu App, S.L.). CaixaBank states "solo facilitamos el acceso al servicio, pero no participamos en su gestión".
  - Price: self-employed €9.56/month or €84.72/year; companies €19.96/month or €191.52/year (ex VAT).
  - TPV Tablet "Incluye el módulo Verifactu de forma gratuita". CaixaBank's TPV services are provided as agent for **Comercia Global Payments** (a CaixaBank and Global Payments JV).
  - Deadlines per the page: 1 Jan 2027 for companies subject to corporate tax, 1 Jul 2027 for the self-employed.
  - No user numbers.

#### Case 8: BBVA (SERES e-invoice financing, Línea Multifinalidad; Holvi covered in Q4)
- **SERES (5 Feb 2025)** — [BBVA, 5 Feb 2025](https://www.bbva.com/es/es/empresas/bbva-y-seres-facilitan-la-financiacion-para-pymes-a-traves-de-facturas-electronicas/) [COMPANY CLAIM]
  - BBVA and SERES (an e-invoicing platform) **partnered** so SMEs can finance issued and received invoices without leaving SERES. Financing runs through BBVA's **Click&Pay** credit limit, with no activation fee, contracted 100% online.
  - Context cited: B2B e-invoices in Spain hit 348,626,876 in H1 2024 (+51.47%).
  - No uptake figures. This is embedded *credit*, not a non-lending tool.
- **Línea Multifinalidad (24 May 2026)** — [Onda Vasca, 24 May 2026](https://www.ondavasca.com/bbva-abre-a-pymes-su-solucion-para-la-gestion-de-tesoreria-y-la-flexibilidad-financiera/amp/) [PRESS]
  - A single credit limit for payroll, social-security contributions, suppliers, taxes, trade finance and receivables advances, with digital drawdown. It is now open to SMEs; previously only firms with turnover above €5m.
  - Credit-led treasury product. No pricing or uptake given.

#### Case 9: Santander (Ebury, cross-border payments and FX for SMEs)
- **30 Apr 2026** — [Santander press release, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.santander.com/en/press-room/press-releases/2026/04/ebury-santanders-international-payments-fintech-secures-550-million-to-accelerate-growth-and-global-expansion) [COMPANY CLAIM]
  - Ebury is raising about £550m, led by Centerbridge, with Santander investing £50m.
  - Santander keeps **55%** and will use the **equity method**, deconsolidating Ebury's revenues and costs.
  - Ebury "has grown its revenues over 30% per annum since Santander invested in 2020" and serves "more than 27,000 businesses worldwide".
  - Payments Solutions targets more than 15% annual revenue growth over 2026–28 and a ~45% EBITDA margin by 2028. Completion is expected by Q1 2027.

### Inferences
- In Spain the non-lending SME engagement play is POS and acquiring plus loyalty (CaixaBank's 31.3% merchant share, POS rebates, Facilitea). Invoicing and accounting are handled through third-party partners on a paid, arm's-length basis (Quipu), not bank-built or free as in the UK (NatWest/FreeAgent, Lloyds Get Paid).
- The Verifactu and e-invoicing mandate (2027 deadlines per CaixaBank) is a forcing event. Banks are positioning partner tools and credit-on-invoice (BBVA–SERES) rather than building accounting. No bank has yet published adoption data.

### Gaps
- No Spanish bank was found publishing user, revenue or retention results for non-lending SME tools.
- [SNIPPET-ONLY] CaixaBank lent over €18bn to SMEs in 2025 (+18%) and grew SME client numbers 15%, from a CaixaBank PDF that was not opened.
- Comercia Global Payments' market share and revenue were not found.
- **Sabadell:** [SNIPPET-ONLY] sold 80% of Paycomet to Nexi for €280m with a 10-year renewable alliance (Feb 2023), and "20% market share in POS terminals". The Sabadell press page returned 403.
- **Santander:** [SNIPPET-ONLY] bought 50.1% of Ebury in 2020 for £350m. [SNIPPET-ONLY] Ebury FY to Apr 2024 revenue was £220.6m (+8%). Getnet SME results were not researched.
- **BBVA:** no evidence found of a BBVA-branded invoicing or Verifactu tool. BBVA Spark not researched. Bankinter, Kutxabank, Unicaja and Sabadell BStartup/Negocios not researched (search budget exhausted).

## Q3. Nordics, Benelux, DACH: accounting firms owned by banks, payment requests, acquiring, ecosystems

### Takeaway
The best-documented *profitable* non-lending SME business found is SpareBank 1 Regnskapshuset SMN, a bank-owned accounting and advisory firm. In 2025 it had NOK 846m revenue, NOK 105.9m PBT (12.5% margin) and 11,245 SME clients, and was explicitly integrated with the owner banks' business advisers through its "Bank i Regnskap" model. ABN AMRO's Tikkie is a scaled payment-request tool: 37,000 business users received more than €930m in 2025. ING's Payvision acquisition (2018) is the clearest failure: wound down in 2021–22 after fraud controversies and an uncompetitive, capital-intensive market. The premise "DNB acquired Fiken" was **not** confirmed. DNB and SpareBank 1 instead invested in the accounting-software maker Uni Micro (details unverified; snippet-only).

### Cited Findings

#### Case 10: SpareBank 1 SMN, SpareBank 1 Regnskapshuset SMN (bank-owned accounting firm; acquired and built through roll-up)
- **2025 annual report** — all from [SpareBank 1 Regnskapshuset SMN Årsmelding 2025](https://www.sparebank1.no/content/dam/SB1/bank/smn/vedlegg/Investor/ars-og-kvartalsrapporter/smn-rh-arsrapport-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
- **Ownership:** a "93,32 % eid datterselskap av SpareBank 1 SMN". SpareBank 1 Gudbrandsdal holds 4.62%, and SpareBank 1 Lom og Skjåk is also an owner.
- **Revenue:** driftsinntekter NOK **845.574m** in 2025 vs NOK 789.163m in 2024 (+7.2%). Advisory revenue grew 1.9%.
- **Profit:** pre-tax profit NOK **105.898m** vs NOK 69.898m (+51%), a "resultatgrad på 12,5 %".
- **Customers and staff:**
  - **11,245 SME customers**; 602 employees; 577 FTEs; 28% are authorised accountants.
  - Average revenue per customer rose from NOK 68,754 (2024) to **NOK 75,196** (2025). Revenue per FTE rose from NOK 1.511m to 1.628m.
- **Model shift:** customers are moving to accounting and payroll *subscriptions*, which made up 22.3% of revenue at year-end, replacing hourly billing. Cloud systems are rolled out across clients.
  - "Bank i Regnskap": a new service model built "i samarbeid med næringslivsrådgivere i eierbankene" (with the owner banks' business advisers) for SMEs with limited admin resources. Implementation was completed in 2025.
- Credit-loss cost on receivables was NOK 1.3m in 2025.
- **Acquisitions:** SR Rekneskap AS and Meese Regnskap AS were merged in from 1 Jan 2025. The firm has offices from Rørvik to Oslo.

#### Case 11: ABN AMRO, Tikkie (built, bank-owned payment-request app with a business version)
- **2025 results** — [Banken.nl, 6 Jan 2026, reporting ABN AMRO's annual Tikkie figures](https://www.banken.nl/nieuws/26709/tikkie-doorbreekt-grens-van-een-miljard-betaalverzoeken-in-recordjaar) [PRESS, from the company release]
  - "ruim 170 miljoen Tikkies" in 2025, worth €8.5bn.
  - Cumulatively it passed 1 billion paid requests. The average Tikkie was €50.03.
  - **Business users "steeg naar 37.000"**, receiving "ruim €930 miljoen aan betalingen". No growth rate was given for business users.

#### Case 12: ING, Payvision (acquired merchant acquirer; **failed, wound down**)
- **Announcement** — [ING press release, 29 Oct 2021](https://ing.com/news/2021/10/ing-phases-out-payvision.html) [COMPANY CLAIM]
  - ING "concluded that it is not feasible to achieve its ambitions with Payvision", given the "rapidly evolving and increasingly competitive and capital-intensive e-commerce merchant market".
  - It aimed to complete the phase-out by Q2 2022.
- **Background** — [American Banker, 1 Nov 2021](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/ing-to-shut-controversial-payments-unit-payvision) [PRESS]
  - ING acquired Payvision in 2018 for about $420m (€360m per other reports).
  - It was under investigation over "processing hundreds of millions of dollars in payments that resulted from illegal investment schemes". EFRI alleged about $160m in fraudulent transactions predating ING's ownership.
  - In 2020 ING sold the gambling and adult-payments portion for €1. Reuters said this removed "high margin" customers and hurt performance.

#### Case 13: KBC (Kate assistant and "beyond banking" services in the KBC Mobile app)
- **Q3 2025** — [KBC 3Q2025 press release](https://www.kbc.com/content/dam/kbccom/doc/newsroom/pressreleases/2025/3q2025-pb-en.pdf) [COMPANY CLAIM, results release]
  - "Kate now autonomously resolves seven out of ten customer queries across our core markets".
  - The Kate Coin programme was enhanced so customers "can earn Kate Coins at one partner and use them at another".
  - Sia named KBC Mobile the world's best mobile banking app (2021, 2024, 2025).
  - **No SME-specific non-banking adoption figures** are disclosed.

### Inferences
- A bank-owned accounting firm (people plus software) can be a standalone profitable business line. SB1 Regnskapshuset SMN earns a 12.5% margin with NOK 75k revenue per client. It is also a distribution and relationship asset ("Bank i Regnskap"). This is a different model from free software bundling (NatWest/FreeAgent).
- Tikkie shows a bank-built payment tool can reach scale. The business segment (37k users, about €930m) is small relative to consumer volume (€8.5bn), i.e. about 11% of value.
- Payvision, like the Barclays partial exit, suggests owning acquiring and e-commerce processing is capital-intensive and risky for banks. Partnering or JVs (CaixaBank–Global Payments, Sabadell–Nexi, Barclays–Brookfield) has become the dominant pattern.

### Gaps
- **DNB / Fiken:** no evidence found that DNB acquired Fiken. DNB lists Fiken only as an integration partner.
  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] DNB (~40%) and SpareBank 1 (~27%) jointly bought Uni Micro in 2020, the platform behind DNB Regnskap and SpareBank 1 Regnskap. Eika and Sparebanken Sør joined as owners in May 2024. The B&R Holding vehicle holds 67%.
  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] "over 40,000 bedriftskunder", expecting 50,000 by year-end. The figures are undated and the Unimicro pages returned headline-only content.
- **ABN AMRO:**
  - [SNIPPET-ONLY] Tikkie business was opened to non-ABN AMRO business account holders in 2025 (ABN AMRO press release, 503 error). ABN AMRO's product page says "40.000 bedrijven" (undated).
  - **New10 closure not confirmed.** [SNIPPET-ONLY] ABN AMRO discontinued "OndernemersKrediet" and points customers to New10 as its online lending channel.
- **ING:** [SNIPPET-ONLY] a €188m goodwill write-down on Payvision in 2020 (Reuters via search snippet). No closure-specific write-off was found.
- **Rabobank:** [SNIPPET-ONLY] partners with Bizcuit (a cash-flow app connected to all Dutch banks) for embedded invoice financing. No usage data; Rabobank's own articles are promotional.

## Q4. France and cross-border exits: Société Générale/Shine, Crédit Agricole/Blank, BBVA/Holvi

### Takeaway
Bank ownership of SME neobanks with invoicing and accounting features has repeatedly ended in exit or consolidation:
- **Société Générale** bought Shine in 2020 (press-cited ~€100m) and sold it to Ageras (agreed Jun 2024, approved Dec 2024). Under 12 months later, the combined business, operating as Shine, was sold to Cegid for a reported **>€1bn**.
- **Crédit Agricole** is closing Blank (announced Jul 2026; migration by Jan 2027) and folding it into a single Pro by CA range tied to its Kolecto platform.
- **BBVA** sold Holvi to its co-founder in 2021.

In each case value accrued outside the bank, or the bank chose simplification over a separate brand.

### Cited Findings

#### Case 14: Société Générale, Shine (acquired 2020, divested 2024)

- **Closure** — all from [MoneyVox, 9 Jul 2026 (citing Les Échos)](https://www.moneyvox.fr/banque-pro/actualites/109623/le-credit-agricole-enterre-blank-sa-banque-en-ligne-pour-les-pro) [PRESS]

### Inferences

## Q5. Failures and shutdowns: what failed and why

### Takeaway
Of the failures found, three are acquisitions later exited (ING/Payvision, BBVA/Holvi, SG/Shine), one is an in-house build closed (CA/Blank), one is a capital-heavy business partially sold (Barclays acquiring), and one is a bank-built venture far below target (NatWest/Mettle vs its 500k ambition). The recurring reasons are capital intensity and competition in acquiring, conduct and fraud risk, sub-scale standalone brands, and group "simplification".

### Cited Findings
- **ING Payvision:** acquired 2018 (~$420m); phase-out announced 29 Oct 2021, completion targeted Q2 2022. Reasons: "not feasible to achieve its ambitions" in a "competitive and capital-intensive e-commerce merchant market", plus investigations over processing payments from illegal investment schemes — [ING, 29 Oct 2021](https://ing.com/news/2021/10/ing-phases-out-payvision.html); [American Banker, 1 Nov 2021](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/ing-to-shut-controversial-payments-unit-payvision)
- **BBVA Holvi:** acquired 2016, sold to its co-founder's vehicle in Feb 2021, terms undisclosed — [BBVA, Feb 2021](https://www.bbva.com/en/bbva-accepts-an-offer-to-sell-holvi-to-keru-fintech-investments/)
- **SG Shine:** acquired 2020, sold to Ageras (exclusivity Jun 2024) — [PR Newswire, 19 Jun 2024](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ageras-enters-into-an-exclusive-agreement-with-societe-generale-to-acquire-shine-and-become-one-of-the-leading-european-providers-of-banking-and-accounting-software-for-smes-302176810.html). Later sold on to Cegid at a reported >€1bn — [Maddyness, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.maddyness.com/2025/11/26/shine-18-mois-apres-son-rachat-par-ageras-la-fintech-bascule-dans-le-giron-de-cegid/)
- **CA Blank:** built 2020, €47m raised in 2023, closure and migration to Pro By CA by Jan 2027 for "une gamme unique, plus simple et plus lisible" — [MoneyVox, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.moneyvox.fr/banque-pro/actualites/109623/le-credit-agricole-enterre-blank-sa-banque-en-ligne-pour-les-pro)
- **Barclays acquiring:** partial carve-out to Brookfield, which could reach ~80%, with a £400m Barclays investment — [PYMNTS, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2025/barclays-sell-stake-payment-acceptance-business-brookfield/)
- **NatWest Mettle (under target):** a 2022 ambition of up to 500k customers within five years and break-even within five years for the BaaS JV — [Euromoney, 14 Nov 2022](https://www.euromoney.com/article/2avw0irwnarql0nxf708w/fintech/natwest-spins-mettle-tech-into-banking-as-a-service-venture/). Actual: about 150k open accounts at end-2025 — [NatWest ARA 2025](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Acquiring and payments:** failures and exits cluster where the business is capital-intensive, has high conduct risk, or depends on scale economies outside the bank's core (Payvision, Barclays).
- **Separate SME neobank brands:** they struggle inside banks (Blank, Shine, Holvi) while standalone specialists consolidating software and banking (Ageras/Shine, Cegid) attract high valuations. Licensed challengers (Monzo, Starling) win on subscriptions.
- **Surviving bank models:** (a) bank-owned accounting firms with a service plus software model (SpareBank 1); (b) free embedded tools that defend the primary account (NatWest/FreeAgent, Lloyds Get Paid); (c) acquiring through JVs or partners (CaixaBank–Global Payments, Sabadell–Nexi, Barclays–Brookfield).

### Gaps
- **HSBC Kinetic closure or fold-back: not found.** The newsroom shows features still added in Jun 2024. [SNIPPET-ONLY] A 2021 HSBC executive quote: "We're trying to avoid concept of a migration".
- **Not verified (no searches left):** NatWest "Bo" (a retail product, so arguably out of scope); NatWest Boxed/Vodeno JV closure; ABN AMRO New10 closure.

## Q6. Evidence that SME tool users have higher balances, retention, primacy or cross-sell

### Takeaway
No bank in this sample published a causal or cohort comparison, i.e. users of an accounting or invoicing tool vs non-users on balances, churn or product holding. The closest proxies are segment-level: Starling's SME primacy (56% vs 35% retail), Monzo's 47% paid-subscriber share among active business users, and SpareBank 1 Regnskapshuset's rising revenue per client alongside a joint bank and accountant service model.

### Cited Findings
- **Starling:** 56% of SME customers use Starling as their primary account (at least £500 a month credited), vs 35% of retail — [Starling, 21 May 2026](https://www.starlingbank.com/news/starling-delivers-fifth-year-of-profitability-and-accelerates-global-growth-strategy) [COMPANY CLAIM]
  - SME deposits were £5.7bn at FY25, down from £6.0bn — [Starling AR 2025](https://www.starlingbank.com/docs/annual-reports/Starling-Bank-Annual-Report-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
- **Monzo:** "47% of monthly active business customers were paid subscribers". Business ARPU grew 16% in FY25 — [Dealroom, 17 Aug 2026](https://dealroom.co/news/145245-monzo-business-reaches-one-million-customers/); [tech.eu, 2 Jun 2025](https://tech.eu/2025/06/02/monzo-reveals-one-third-of-customers-use-it-as-a-primary-bank-as-profits-and-revenues-swell/) [PRESS]
- **SpareBank 1 Regnskapshuset SMN:** revenue per SME client rose from NOK 68,754 to NOK 75,196 (2024 to 2025). It runs the "Bank i Regnskap" model jointly with the owner banks' business advisers — [Årsmelding 2025](https://www.sparebank1.no/content/dam/SB1/bank/smn/vedlegg/Investor/ars-og-kvartalsrapporter/smn-rh-arsrapport-2025.pdf) [AUDITED/REPORTED]
- **NatWest:** links Mettle's service ranking (2nd in the CMA survey) to its tool set (invoicing, receipts, FreeAgent). No balance or retention metrics are given — [NatWest ARA 2025](https://investors.natwestgroup.com/~/media/Files/R/RBS-IR-V2/results-center/13022026/nwg-annual-report-and-accounts-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The data supports "tools correlate with engaged, paying, primary SME relationships" at challengers. It does not isolate the effect of the tools themselves.
- For a course case study, the honest framing is that banks market tools as primacy and retention levers, but almost none publish the uplift.

### Gaps
- No published cohort data from any bank in the sample on deposits, churn, NPS or credit performance for tool users vs non-users.
- [SNIPPET-ONLY] Mettle's 2022 customer growth "correlates with a 600% increase in deposits" (Finextra, Mar 2022, 403). This is the only quasi-link found, and it is a company-sourced correlation, not a cohort study.
- Credit-performance benefits of using accounting data (e.g. FreeAgent or Unimicro data feeding underwriting) were not documented in any source opened.
